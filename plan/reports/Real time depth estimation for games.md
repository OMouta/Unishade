# Estimated motion, not bigger models, steadies depth

The largest improvement available to Unishade's Depth add-on is a motion field estimated from color and used everywhere, because the sources agree that its weak points are temporal filtering, normalization and edge handling, and that the model it runs is still the right one. Depth Anything V2 Small remains the only widely exported small foundation model under Apache-2.0, and every stronger real-time candidate published since is research-only, non-commercial, softer at edges, or not yet downloadable. Every comparable open tool smooths only a normalization range or blends whole depth maps with no motion compensation, so per-pixel filtering along motion is unbuilt work that a compute-shader block matcher makes possible. The same motion field is what DLSS 5 needs most. Third-party A/B tests put the value of exact depth to DLSS 5 at **0.03 dB** and the gap between a coarse motion estimate and a good one at **3.1 dB**, so the DLSS5 add-on's placeholder motion vectors are the urgent problem and its placeholder depth is close to harmless. Under the model, DirectML stopped at 1.15.4 in October 2024, and NVIDIA's TensorRT for RTX provider is the only documented faster runtime that still shares D3D12 resources, at about 111 MB of extra download under a proprietary licence. Nothing here was measured on Windows hardware, and no public source times any of these techniques on a mid-range GPU that a game is already loading, so the closing recommendations separate what sources support from what Unishade has to time itself. The licence position suits an MIT project that downloads third-party files on demand, with one hard limit. No public licence covers redistributing `nvngx_dlssnr.dll`.

## Depth Anything V2 Small stays, and the runtime under it is the bigger lever

Two years of new models have not produced a small one that Unishade can both ship and prefer. Depth Anything V2 Small has 24.8M parameters and an Apache-2.0 licence, while its Base and Large siblings are CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([Depth Anything V2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/README.md)). Depth Anything 3 added Apache-2.0 Small and Base checkpoints, but they are multi-view models run on a single image, and its Apache-2.0 monocular checkpoint exists only at ViT-L size ([Depth Anything 3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3/main/README.md)). Apple's Depth Pro 2, released on 19 November 2026, includes a 24.3M-parameter S model that handles native-resolution depth and causal video streaming, and its weights are research-use only ([Depth Pro 2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26603v1), [apple/DepthPro2](https://huggingface.co/apple/DepthPro2)). The Depth Anything 3 small models are a poor trade for a ReShade depth buffer because edge quality is what shaders consume. In the MoGe-3 paper's averaged boundary F1, the any-view Depth Anything 3 scores 6.61 against 15.6 for MoGe-2 and 16.3 for Depth Pro ([MoGe-3 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17967v2)), and Depth Anything V2 scores 13.9, 4.74, 32.5 and 6.10 on the four sets that MoGe-2 reports ([MoGe-2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02546)). No source publishes a boundary metric for any small model, so DAv2-S against DA3-Small on edges is a test Unishade has to run. DA3Mono-Large is the one legal upgrade with evidence behind it. Its distilled monocular student reaches delta1 82.3 on Sintel against 77.2 for Depth Anything V2 ([DA3 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10647v1)), and the DirectML timings in the table show a high-end Radeon running it at 252 to 364 px at rates a reprojection scheme can use.

Raising the input size is not the way to buy quality. On Middlebury, oracle-scaled AbsRel for DAv2-S goes from **8.93% at 518x770 to 9.17% at 1008x1512** while Jetson GPU time rises from 11.5 to 76.6 ms, and the authors conclude that monocular error does not improve with input size ([Orin benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)). The model's authors do say that higher test resolution sharpens the prediction ([Depth Anything V2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09414v1)), so the 924 preset buys edge detail only. With 14-pixel patches on a 16:9 frame, my arithmetic gives 777 tokens at a 518 longest side and 2,442 at 924, which is about 3.1 times the work for a benefit that a better upsampler can supply at shader cost. That makes 924 the preset to question once the upsampler improves.

Game footage is the model's weak domain, and data is the remedy. Depth Anything V2 Small ranks cartoon and painted images well, at 98.4% on the DA-2K non-real split ([Depth Anything V2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09414v1)). Yet on originlab/game-depth, which pairs 48,615 frames from 10 commercial games with engine z-buffers, Depth Anything V2 scores AbsRel 0.098 against **0.050 for a model trained on 17,799 game frames** ([originlab/game-depth](https://huggingface.co/datasets/originlab/game-depth)). That comparison favours the in-domain model by construction and the dataset forbids commercial use and redistribution, so read it as a ceiling on what game data can buy. A hobbyist has already distilled a game-tuned DAv2 student from first-person shooter screenshots, under CC-BY-NC-4.0 and with no benchmarks ([Ultimate DAV2 model card](https://huggingface.co/Jokeru1983/Ultimate_DAV2_ViT-S_Gaming)). Nobody has published a small student trained on game captures with real depth buffers as labels and a permissive licence.

### The same model runs four times faster or slower depending on the runtime

Published latency for this model spans a wide range on similar hardware. Depth Anything Small takes 3 ms at 518x518 under TensorRT FP16 on an RTX 4090 including pre and post-processing ([depth-anything-tensorrt](https://github.com/spacewalk01/depth-anything-tensorrt)), and 13.3 ms under ONNX Runtime on an RTX 4080 ([Depth-Anything-ONNX issue 26](https://github.com/fabio-sim/Depth-Anything-ONNX/issues/26)). A profile on an RTX 3070 explains the spread. FP32 eager PyTorch takes 19.13 ms, FP16 takes 10.92 ms, with attention moving to a fused kernel and dropping from 7.1 to 1.4 ms, and capturing the graph to remove per-kernel launch overhead brings it to **6.86 ms** ([realtime-depth-optimization](https://github.com/karadjordjepetrovic/realtime-depth-optimization)). No source publishes a DirectML latency for Depth Anything V2 Small with a named GPU, resolution and precision. Unishade's own numbers would be the first.

DirectML will not get faster. Microsoft describes it as in sustained engineering with new development moved to Windows ML ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/directml/dml)), the newest DirectML package is 1.15.4 from 28 October 2024 ([NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/Microsoft.AI.DirectML/)), and the last DirectML build of ONNX Runtime is 1.24.4 while mainline is at 1.32.0 ([NuGet](https://www.nuget.org/packages/Microsoft.ML.OnnxRuntime.DirectML)). Unishade already runs the final version of both. The one successor with a documented way to bind app-owned D3D12 resources is NVIDIA's TensorRT for RTX execution provider. It supports RTX 30 series and newer ([ONNX Runtime docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/TensorRTRTX-ExecutionProvider.html)), ONNX Runtime 1.25.0 added D3D12 external resource import for it ([ORT 1.25.0 notes](https://newreleases.io/project/pypi/onnxruntime/release/1.25.0)), and the interop API creates tensors on imported memory without copying and synchronizes through D3D12 fences ([OrtInteropApi](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/api/c/struct_ort_interop_api.html)). NVIDIA claims 50% higher throughput than DirectML ([NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/deploy-ai-models-faster-with-windows-ml-on-rtx-pcs)). The provider is 110.9 MB compressed and 286 MB unpacked ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/onnxruntime-ep-nv-tensorrt-rtx-cu13/)). The case for it rests on vendor claims and API reference pages. No independent benchmark compares it with DirectML on a vision transformer, and no public code shows its D3D12 import path working.

AMD and Intel users have no documented faster zero-copy path than DirectML today. Microsoft's CGC backend compiles models for an app-chosen D3D12 device with vendor compilers, but it is experimental and its validated list contains no DINOv2 or DPT model ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/ai/new-windows-ml/runtime/model-compilation-execution)). Gains on those cards come from lower resolution, a clean export and scheduling.

Several cheap checks follow from other projects' bugs. A dynamic-axes export of Depth Anything 3 made the position-embedding Resize fall back to the CPU under DirectML, at 342 ms per depth map against 55 ms for a fixed-shape export ([SyLC model card](https://huggingface.co/Symphoenix/sylc_TRT)). Pinning shapes on ONNX Runtime 1.24 through 1.27 crashed or produced wrong depth for one Depth Anything 3 export ([ONNX card](https://huggingface.co/Jens-Duttke/Depth-Anything-3-MONO-ONNX)), and Unishade runs 1.24.4 with fixed shapes. An FP16 TensorRT engine of DAv2-S at full resolution deviated 17.5% from FP32 without any error ([Orin benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)). INT8 is a dead end for now, since a user measured DAv2-S at 518 running at 41.3 FPS in INT8 against 127 FPS in FP16 on an RTX A2000 ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/int8-throughput-and-latency-worse-than-fp16-for-midas-dpt-hybrid-model-on-thor/354915)). Token pruning on DPT-style depth showed a wall-clock speedup of 1.12 times at one resolution only ([arXiv 2609.27988](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27988)).

## Comparable tools smooth a range, and none filters depth along motion

Unishade's Depth add-on has no direct peer inside ReShade. Repository searches for ReShade combined with monocular depth returned no project that generates depth with a network ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=reshade+depth+estimation&type=repositories)). The reference implementations are the open 2D-to-3D desktop tools, and both made the model choice Unishade did. iw3's real-time mode defaults to Depth Anything V2 Small at a 392 px short side ([iw3 desktop docs](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/docs/desktop.md), [depth_anything_model.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/depth_models/depth_anything_model.py)), and desktop2stereo defaults to depth resolution 336 ([settings.yaml](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/settings.yaml)). Commercial products publish requirements and frame rates with no engineering detail. Viture, for one, lists 1080p at 35 FPS on an RTX 4060 Laptop in its quality mode and says nothing about the model or its filtering ([Viture guide](https://www.viture.com/en-US/academy/immersive-3d/desktop)).

Their temporal handling is thin, which is the opening for Unishade. iw3 smooths two scalars per frame, the minimum and maximum of the depth map, with an exponential moving average that the GUI calls flicker reduction and preselects at decay 0.9 ([depth_scaler.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/depth_scaler.py), [gui_shared.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/desktop/gui_shared.py)). desktop2stereo clips each frame to its 2nd and 98th percentile, then blends whole depth maps per pixel at 10% per frame with no motion compensation and no reset on cuts ([depth.py](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/depth.py)). Neither reprojects the previous depth along motion before blending. Unishade's smoothed peak with blend 0.2 belongs to the same family as iw3's max-mode scaler and shares its documented failure. The iw3 author's diagnosis of flicker on snow and sand is that "the particles are closest objects to the camera, causing the maximum disparity to flicker", and he proposes the median as the reference value ([nunif issue 583](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/583)). A weapon model, a particle or a HUD icon does the same to a peak.

### Scale and shift jitter is most of the flicker

Research on streaming depth points at normalization before per-pixel noise. DyFN, a 2026 method that stabilizes a frozen Depth Anything V2 with a small causal ConvGRU, loses most of its gain when its scale-alignment loss is removed, with Sintel AbsRel rising from 0.180 to 0.245, and loses almost nothing without its temporal loss, at 0.183 ([DyFN paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.25308)). oVDA scores KITTI AbsRel 0.103 with global alignment and 0.140 when scale and shift are fixed from the first frame, which shows how much of a streaming model's error is drift ([oVDA paper](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.09182)). Video Depth Anything keeps one scale and shift per video and stitches clips by a least-squares fit in inverse depth over the overlap frames ([VDA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12375v3)). VDPP found the median a better per-frame scale statistic than the mean ([VDPP paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06665)).

The two parameters do different damage. An unknown scale stretches the scene uniformly and distorts no shape, while a shift changes shape in ways that only show when the geometry is viewed obliquely ([Yin et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.09365)). For a ReShade consumer, scale jitter appears as a pulsing ambient occlusion radius and pumping fog, and shift error tilts floors and flattens distant surfaces. The supported design is therefore to track both parameters, estimate them from many pixels with percentiles or a trimmed least-squares fit against the motion-warped previous output, smooth them inside iw3's recommended 0.75 to 0.99 decay range ([iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)), and reset them on scene cuts. A fit against the previous output is recursive and will drift, so it needs a slow pull back toward the raw model output. No source gives a tested recipe for this on games.

### Learned video depth exists, and only one causal model is shippable

Three learned models are causal and fast enough to matter, and one has a clean licence. Video Depth Anything Small gained an experimental streaming mode in July 2025 that reuses the offline weights and caches temporal-attention states, at the cost of ScanNet delta1 falling from **0.926 to 0.836**, with the performance-gap issue and the ONNX export requests all still open ([VDA README](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Video-Depth-Anything), [VDA issues](https://github.com/DepthAnything/Video-Depth-Anything/issues)). oVDA fine-tunes the same head for causal use with a bounded 16-frame cache and reaches 20 FPS in FP16 on a Jetson Orin NX, but its weights prohibit commercial use ([oVDA paper](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.09182), [model card](https://huggingface.co/FriedFeid/oVDA)). FlashDepth is Apache-2.0 but depends on a locally built Mamba package, its Windows build issue has been open since June 2025, and users filed issues about flicker ([FlashDepth GitHub](https://github.com/Eyeline-Research/FlashDepth), [issues](https://github.com/Eyeline-Research/FlashDepth/issues)). The one learned stabilizer with MIT weights, NVDS, damaged a modern backbone in an independent test. Applied to Depth Anything V2 Large it dropped KITTI delta1 from 0.815 to 0.617 and worsened the temporal alignment error from 1.140 to 2.536 ([VDA paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12375v3)). Diffusion video models and learned G-buffer estimators such as DiffusionRenderer and RGBX-Next sit far outside a per-frame budget, with DepthCrafter at about 2.1 fps on an A100 under a research-only licence ([DepthCrafter README](https://github.com/Tencent/DepthCrafter), [RGBX-Next](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13929)).

The newest work still shows where small models are heading. Depth Pro 2 reports temporal jitter of 1.39 on Sintel for its largest model against 11.26 for the original Depth Pro, from a design whose smallest variant is a DINOv3 ViT-S with a 2.7M-parameter decoder ([Depth Pro 2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26603v1)). Those jitter figures belong to the largest model and none are published for the S size, so a stable 24M-parameter model is designed and released but not yet proven, and no open-licence equivalent has shipped.

### Propagating depth along flow is more stable than re-estimating it

VeloDepth is the published proof that depth can run below display rate. It runs the full model on about one frame in thirty, warps the previous depth and features forward with optical flow, corrects them with a learned residual, and scores a consistency of 87.5 against 80.7 for Video Depth Anything and 74.5 for per-frame Depth Anything V2 at 26.2 FPS on an RTX 3090 ([VeloDepth paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10725)). Its most useful result for Unishade is that the flow source barely matters. CPU DIS flow gives 91.3 accuracy and 87.3 consistency against 91.4 and 87.2 for RAFT, and codec motion vectors come close ([VeloDepth paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10725)). Cheap flow is good enough for depth. The repository is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so only the idea transfers ([VeloDepth GitHub](https://github.com/lpiccinelli-eth/velodepth)). Its stated limits are visible jumps at key frames and degradation under large motion.

Practitioners are converging on the same plan from the other side. An iw3 user running a heavier streaming model went from about 23 to about 42 FPS by reusing depth on alternate frames, and the author's plan is to warp the previous depth forward with hardware optical flow, with the caveat that for a small model skipping frames saves only 10 to 20% of his pipeline ([nunif issue 619](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/619)). AsyncMDE pairs a 3.83M-parameter fast path at 237 FPS with a 97.5M model refreshing in the background ([AsyncMDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10438)). The pattern pays off when the model is heavier than ViT-S or when the display runs at 120 to 240 Hz, which no learned video model reaches per frame.

The rendering literature supplies the filter structure, with one adjustment that is my reasoning and has no published test. Temporal anti-aliasing splits into sample accumulation and history validation ([Yang, Liu and Salvi](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/yang2020survey)), and SVGF falls back to a spatial estimate on disocclusion ([SVGF preprint](https://cg.ivd.kit.edu/publications/2017/svgf/svgf_preprint.pdf)). Color TAA clamps history to the current frame's neighbourhood because the current frame is trustworthy. With network depth the current frame is the thing that flickers, so a tight clamp would reintroduce the flicker. The rejection signals should be disocclusion, color disagreement at the reprojected position and large relative depth disagreement, and the blend should happen in inverse depth, the space the model predicts. No paper or project applies TAA-style accumulation to monocular network depth and reports numbers. One caution on scoring it comes from Khan et al., who note that the flow-warping error metric "is minimized when depth variance is low", so an over-smoothed result scores well while being wrong ([Khan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07435v1)).

Cuts need a reset, and games need a cheaper detector than film tools use. iw3 runs a shot-boundary network and resets its normalization and model state at each cut, while its author doubts scene detection will hold up in games where large score displays update every frame ([iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md), [nunif issue 619](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/619)). AMD's optical flow compares luminance histograms of nine screen sections with the previous frame and returns zero motion when the change passes a threshold ([FidelityFX Optical Flow manual](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/optical-flow/)). That sectioned test is the better fit for a HUD-heavy frame.

The shader route is the base case because it works on every vendor and has measured quality. DLSS5-Feeder's block matcher reports errors of 0.48 px on textured and 0.57 px on plain moving objects against 0.84 and 0.96 px for LumeniteFX on the same synthetic scene ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)). AMD's optical flow is the low-effort start since it ships as a D3D12 compute component under MIT, but its integer 8x8 output rounds away the sub-pixel motion that slow pans consist of. The ReShade shaders share a design worth reimplementing from scratch, a mip pyramid of a small per-pixel feature with a few candidates per level seeded from the coarser level and from the previous frame. Their code cannot enter an MIT project. One of them, the motion header in vort_Shaders, is CC BY-NC 4.0 inside an otherwise MIT repository ([vort_MotionVectors.fxh](https://github.com/vortigern11/vort_Shaders/blob/HEAD/Shaders/Includes/vort_MotionVectors.fxh)), which is the kind of detail to check before borrowing. CShade's Lucas-Kanade flow is the BSD-3-Clause exception ([CShade](https://github.com/papadanku/CShade)). Several of these shaders match on depth as well as color. Unishade should match on luma only, since its depth is the noisy signal the motion is meant to steady.

NVIDIA's hardware engine is the quality option with two open questions. Its documentation was last updated in February 2023 and lists Turing, Ampere and Ada with no mention of RTX 50 ([application note](https://docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/optical-flow-sdk/nvofa-application-note)), which matters because DLSS 5 officially requires RTX 50. A community Vulkan layer already feeds `VK_NV_optical_flow` output into DLSS 5 ([DLSS5VKLayer](https://github.com/bmitch87/DLSS5VKLayer)), but reaching that extension from a D3D12 application through shared textures is an untested route. A second network for flow is the wrong trade. The iw3 author observes that learned optical flow "can be as heavy as, or even heavier than, small depth estimation model" ([nunif issue 619](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/619)), and no flow network has a published single-digit millisecond time at 540p on a mid-range card.

Confidence has to travel with every vector. The standard occlusion test is forward-backward consistency ([UnFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07837)), and the cheaper stand-in is to warp the previous color by the vector and compare it with the current color. DLSS5-Feeder's validation adds the rule that matters most for capture. A pixel whose neighbourhood did not change gets exactly zero, the static decision needs two wins in a row because deciding afresh each frame made vectors alternate, and failed vectors are zeroed and flagged in a mask ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)). Raw luma matching also fails on lighting changes, where optical flow "answers a lighting change with a wrong-but-confident vector" ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)). The census transform compensates for additive and multiplicative illumination changes ([UnFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07837)), which makes it the matching cost to try for exposure adaptation, flashes and bloom.

## DLSS 5 forgives fake depth and punishes bad motion

DLSS 5 is a same-resolution neural relighting pass that runs after upscaling. NVIDIA's public description names color and motion vectors as its inputs and never mentions depth ([NVIDIA, 1 September 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)). The public SDK exposes only the anonymous slot `NVSDK_NGX_Feature_Reserved18` with no header or guide ([nvsdk_ngx_defs.h](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/include/nvsdk_ngx_defs.h)). The best primary description of the real interface is NVIDIA's own RTX Remix source, which passes color, output, motion vectors, depth and a control mask with `InDepthInverted = 0`, and passes no jitter, camera matrices, near or far planes ([dxvk-remix NGX wrapper](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp)). Remix tone maps to SDR before the call and inverts afterwards, with the comment that the feature "operates in SDR space" ([dxvk-remix rtx_context.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_context.cpp)). A capture pipeline therefore hands the model what it expects in color space and loses nothing by lacking jitter and camera data. That is why DLSS 5 transfers to captured frames while DLSS Super Resolution does not. DLSS5-Feeder could not add a super resolution mode on finished frames because they carry no sub-pixel jitter ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)), and on video DLSS Super Resolution scored 74.9 VMAF against bicubic's 80.5 ([DLSS 5 Video Player](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player)).

The only careful measurements of the depth input say it hardly matters. With true depth, a heuristic proxy or a constant 0.75, outputs "sit within 0.03 dB, 0.002 SSIM" of each other, and the one signal found was a rise in false-motion rate from 0.0605 to 0.0759 on an interior clip with constant depth ([guide files report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/guide-files-20260924/REPORT.md)). The channel is live, since proxy and constant depth produce different output digests on all eight test clips, but the authors found "no visible quality win" ([depth A/B report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/depth-ab-20260914/REPORT.md)). Two limits apply. Those tests passed depth through a 12-pixel grid, so they bound what coarse depth can do, and nobody has published DLSS 5 results with a learned monocular depth map at full resolution. I read this as low risk and low reward. Feeding Depth Anything output will not hurt, and effort on depth should be justified by ReShade shaders.

Motion is where quality is won or lost. On a layered pan, a CPU block matcher on a coarse grid cost **3.1 dB and 0.078 SSIM** against NVIDIA's hardware optical flow, and exact motion added only 0.13 dB more ([guide files report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/guide-files-20260924/REPORT.md)). In DLSS5-Feeder's test scene, output with no vectors is 1.38 times as restless as the input for DLAA and 1.54 times with the neural pass, against about 1.04 to 1.08 with estimated vectors, and its author concludes "the vectors matter more with a neural pass, not less" ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)). Zero vectors while the camera moves are the worst case. A Stray add-on that passed zero motion for the static world saw "ghosting and over-smoothing that accumulates over seconds" ([stray-dlssnr](https://github.com/lunks/stray-dlssnr)). Placeholder motion vectors in Unishade's DLSS5 add-on produce exactly this whenever the camera moves. The opposite error also costs. On a held frame, a small spurious flow field made sharpness creep from 1.03 to 1.12 times the source over two seconds, and a test that snaps ambiguous vectors to zero removed it for 0.06 to 0.18 ms at 1080p ([zero-motion gate report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/neural-mv-gate-20260924/REPORT.md)).

The conventions are settled enough to implement, with the sign unconfirmed by NVIDIA for this feature. DLSS motion vectors map a pixel to its previous position, in pixels, in RG16F or RG32F with the origin at the upper left, and DLSS depth assumes near at 0.0 and far at 1.0 unless the inverted flag is set ([DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)). Remix passes render-resolution vectors "in render-pixel units" with scale 1, 1 and gives the motion texture its own subrect ([dxvk-remix neural rendering](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.cpp)). Unishade's R16G16_FLOAT texture with scale 1 matches that. Normalized inverse depth is already an affine image of a reversed-Z buffer, so the depth texture needs only a flag that agrees with what it stores, near at 0 with `DepthInverted = 0` or near at 1 with the flag set. No near or far plane has to be invented because DLSS 5 never receives one. Four implementation details come from other callers. NGX silently ignores parameters it does not recognise ([OpenRemix header](https://github.com/OpenRenderCollective/OpenRemix/blob/HEAD/src/native/upscale/nvsdk_ngx_defs_dlssnr.h)), so a misspelled key fails without an error. A typeless planar depth format is the likely cause of an unsupported-format failure ([stray-dlssnr](https://github.com/lunks/stray-dlssnr)). A D3D12 caller always binds a depth texture because that path rejects null resources ([depth A/B report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/depth-ab-20260914/REPORT.md)). And motion should be estimated from the raw captured frames, never from the model's output, to avoid a feedback loop ([ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR](https://github.com/lisitskyaa/ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR/blob/HEAD/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)).

Two capture-specific behaviours need handling. Thirty frames per second content shown at 60 Hz makes a temporal model flicker at 30 Hz because it does not return the same output for a repeated input, and DLSS5-Feeder fixes it by detecting repeated frames and showing the earlier output again ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)). The HUD is processed with the scene in every capture-based tool, a limit the Magpie fork that ships this feature states outright ([magpie-dlss5-amd](https://github.com/A-ENTROPY/magpie-dlss5-amd)). The best published countermeasure marks a pixel as HUD when it stands still while at least a quarter of the frame and its own surroundings move and it has the contrast of lettering, then restores the original pixel, which cut HUD error from 1.12 to 0.43 in one test ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)). The DLL also holds `DLSSNR.UI`, `UIAlpha`, `UICorrection` and `ControlMask` keys, and their semantics are undocumented ([OpenRemix header](https://github.com/OpenRenderCollective/OpenRemix/blob/HEAD/src/native/upscale/nvsdk_ngx_defs_dlssnr.h)).

Cost and hardware set the audience. On an RTX 5090 with the 310.9.16 DLL the original network costs 3.78 ms at 1080p and 9.63 ms at 4K, a lighter second network costs 2.42 and 6.04 ms, and peak process VRAM is about 1.1 GiB at 1080p ([runtime report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/runtime-310916-20261031/REPORT.md)). Build 310.8.0, the one Unishade calls, renders the same output for every preset value, so it offers only the original network ([runtime report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/runtime-310916-20261031/REPORT.md)), and it rejects every GPU architecture below Blackwell ([OpenRemix header](https://github.com/OpenRenderCollective/OpenRemix/blob/HEAD/src/native/upscale/nvsdk_ngx_defs_dlssnr.h)).

## Shaders need one-pixel silhouettes, 32-bit storage and a HUD policy

ReShade effects differentiate the depth buffer, so defects that a stereo warp hides become visible geometry. The blur and dilation recipes of the 2D-to-3D tools would flatten the contact detail that ambient occlusion needs. Unishade needs the engine practice for half-resolution effects.

The current joint bilateral upsample is the classic 2007 technique ([JBU project page](https://johanneskopf.de/publications/jbu/)) and it has a known side effect on sloped surfaces. A color edge skews the filter weights to one side, the weighted centroid moves, and the output takes the depth of a point up to a model pixel away, so a textured floor acquires ripples that finite-difference normals turn into embossing. Game engines avoid this by using the guide only where it is needed. Mikkel Gjoel's fog upsampler for INSIDE averages its four low-resolution taps whenever their depths agree within `(max - min) / avg < 0.1` and applies depth-aware weights only at discontinuities ([pixelmager](https://pixelmager.github.io/depth_aware_upsampling/upsampling)). The guided filter is no substitute at silhouettes, since Bart Wronski's analysis lists smudging and halos near strong discontinuities among its failures ([Wronski](https://bartwronski.com/2019/09/22/local-linear-models-guided-filter/)). The equivalent for Unishade is a two-branch pass. Where the 3x3 low-resolution neighbourhood has no discontinuity, interpolate with no color taps at all. Where it has one, split the taps into a near and a far cluster, pick the cluster whose color is closer to the full-resolution pixel, and output that cluster's depth. This removes the intermediate values that monocular models produce at edges, which a Jetson benchmark counts as flying points on **25.8% of edge pixels** for DAv2-S ([Orin benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)). The design is assembled from the cited practice and is untested. For scale, Intel's XeGTAO runs a full three-pass ambient occlusion in about 1.4 ms at 4K on an RTX 3070 ([XeGTAO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO/master/README.md)), so a single upsample pass is cheap by comparison. Learned upsamplers are out of budget, with AnyUp at about 20.6 ms for a 448x448 output ([AnyUp](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12764v2)).

One-pixel silhouettes are what make the shaders' own normal reconstruction work. The accurate five-tap scheme extrapolates two taps per side toward the centre and keeps the side that agrees, which removes artifacts at depth discontinuities ([Yuwen Wu](https://atyuwen.github.io/posts/normal-reconstruction)), and qUINT MXAO keeps the position delta with the smaller depth difference on each axis ([qUINT_mxao.fx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/martymcmodding/qUINT/master/Shaders/qUINT_mxao.fx)). Both assume an edge one pixel wide. A depth ramp several pixels wide from a smooth upsample defeats them, and the normal points sideways along the whole silhouette band. Until edges are snapped, normal-free effects are the safe recommendation to users. Glamarye's fast ambient occlusion ignores normals, skips pixels at depth 0 as menus and depth 1 as sky, and is MIT ([Glamarye](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rj200/Glamarye_Fast_Effects_for_ReShade/main/README.md)).

The mapping to ReShade's convention deserves a check against the header. `GetLinearizedDepth` defaults to a far plane of 1000 and a reversed input and computes `depth /= FAR - depth * (FAR - N)` with `N = 1` ([ReShade.fxh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crosire/reshade-shaders/slim/Shaders/ReShade.fxh)). Worked through, the stored value a shader expects is affine in inverse distance, so a buffer written from the model's disparity can be interpolated exactly on planes, provided the add-on's mapping matches that formula. MXAO reconstructs position with no field of view or aspect correction ([qUINT_mxao.fx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/martymcmodding/qUINT/master/Shaders/qUINT_mxao.fx)), so the right depth scale depends on the consuming shader, and a user-facing depth strength control is the practical answer. Sky needs a decision too. Depth Anything's training sets sky disparity to 0, the farthest value ([Depth Anything paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10891v1)), and only the Large Depth Anything 3 mono and metric models output a sky mask ([DA3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3/main/README.md)). Whether DAv2-S pins the farthest wall of an indoor game scene to zero disparity, which would stretch the back of every room, is an empirical question that a fit against real game depth answers.

Precision is the least sourced item here and possibly the most visible. A half float has 11 significant bits, and by my arithmetic one quantization step between adjacent pixels on a camera-facing wall at 2560 px across a 90 degree field of view tilts the reconstructed normal by tens of degrees, which would appear as contour-line terraces in ambient occlusion. No source measures this for fp16 monocular depth output. The supporting evidence is indirect, in that XeGTAO notes its 16-bit float depth "causes minor precision issues" and is incompatible with its built-in normal generator ([XeGTAO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO/master/README.md)). The check costs one capture of a flat wall and a normal debug view. If terraces appear, the fixes are an fp32 final layer through mixed-precision export, a small plane fit at model resolution, and R32F storage throughout.

The HUD has no published automatic solution for capture-only tools. ReShade practice is a user-painted mask applied at the top and bottom of the effect chain ([UIMask thread](https://reshade.me/forum/shader-presentation/3006-i-ported-remade-uimask-for-3-0)). For Unishade the static-pixel detector described above is the best starting point, with a painted mask as the fallback for semi-transparent panels. HUD pixels should be excluded from the normalization statistics and the scale and shift fit, and their depth filled from the surrounding scene so a near-plane sticker does not cast occlusion onto the world.

Ground truth is obtainable, which makes all of this tunable. OmniWorld-Game captured about 18.5M frames by reading depth through ReShade during rendering while OBS recorded synchronized color ([OmniWorld](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12201v1)). The same method gives Unishade aligned pairs with the HUD on, at its real output resolution, from games it is allowed to capture. The established metrics are scale-and-shift alignment in disparity, Depth Pro's boundary F1 from depth-ratio contours ([Depth Pro](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02073v1)), and the flying-points rate ([Orin benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)). No benchmark scores monocular depth by the ambient occlusion computed from it, and that comparison, the same shader on real and estimated depth, is the number that tracks what users see. DLSS5-Feeder's synthetic test rig with exact vectors is the matching tool for motion ([DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)).

## What to change first, what to prototype, what to avoid

The order below puts cheap changes with direct support first, then the shared motion module, then the experiments whose payoff depends on numbers nobody has.

| Order | Change | What supports it | What Unishade must measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build a test rig. Capture color with real ReShade depth, add a synthetic scene with known motion, and time the current add-on at each preset with the GPU idle and under a game | OmniWorld's capture method, DLSS5-Feeder's test rig, and the absence of any public DirectML timing for this model | Everything below |
| 2 | Replace the smoothed peak with near and far anchors from percentiles, track scale and shift, smooth them, reset on cuts, exclude HUD pixels | iw3 author's diagnosis, desktop2stereo's 2/98 percentile clip, VDPP's median result, DyFN's ablation | Percentile and decay values, the residual shift of DAv2-S on game frames |
| 3 | Decouple inference from presentation. Run it on its own queue, never wait on its fence in the present path, reuse the last finished depth | iw3 and desktop2stereo already run depth out of step with the display. No source measures the alternative | Game frame-time impact at different queue priorities |
| 4 | Fix the DLSS5 add-on's contract. Bind a real R32_FLOAT depth texture whose inversion flag matches its contents, set reset on cuts, detect repeated frames, verify each key by output difference | Remix source, the D3D12 null-resource finding, the silent-ignore finding, the repeated-frame finding | Motion vector sign on a synthetic pan |
| 5 | Add a color-only motion estimator with confidence. Start from FidelityFX Optical Flow or write a pyramid block matcher at half resolution with sub-pixel refinement, a static snap-to-zero test with hysteresis, a reprojection check and a scene-change flag | AMD's MIT component, DLSS5-Feeder's measured errors, the zero-motion gate report | Cost per vendor at 1080p and 4K capture, flow alone |
| 6 | Feed those vectors to DLSS 5 as current-to-previous pixels in RG16F, estimated from raw captured frames, sampled with nearest fetch | The 3.1 dB result, the restlessness results, the Stray report | Whether low-resolution vectors with a subrect behave like full-resolution ones |
| 7 | Filter depth along motion. Warp the previous depth, blend in inverse depth, reject on disocclusion and color mismatch, blend new inferences in instead of swapping | VeloDepth's consistency gain, the TAA and SVGF structure | The whole design. No published precedent on monocular depth |
| 8 | Replace the joint bilateral upsample with the two-branch pass, store depth in R32F, check for terracing | Gjoel's upsampler, the flying-points data, XeGTAO's precision note | Thresholds, boundary F1 and normal error against real depth |

Four prototypes are worth the time once that base exists. The first is the TensorRT for RTX provider on RTX 30 and newer as an on-demand download, with fixed-shape FP16 engines validated numerically against FP32 per preset and a cache per GPU and runtime version. The open questions are whether one onnxruntime.dll newer than 1.24.4 can host both DirectML and the plugin, and whether NVIDIA's CUDA-in-graphics mode helps or hurts, given NVIDIA's own statement that its shared-memory limits "will generally result in reduced performance compared to running standalone inference" ([NVIDIA docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/tensorrt-rtx/latest/inference-library/compute-graphics.html)). The second is NVIDIA hardware optical flow through `VK_NV_optical_flow`, which first requires confirming on a real RTX 50 card that the engine exists there. The third is a quality tier that runs DA3Mono-Large at 252 to 364 px every few frames with flow reprojection in between, since without VeloDepth's learned residual the usable interval is unknown and probably a few frames. The fourth is longer term, a ViT-S student fine-tuned on Unishade's own game captures with real depth labels, which the originlab result suggests could roughly halve relative error. A streaming export of Video Depth Anything Small with state tensors as inputs and outputs is a fifth candidate, held back by its measured accuracy drop in streaming mode. Running inference on an integrated GPU is a sixth, with dual-GPU Lossless Scaling setups as the precedent ([Lossless Scaling dual GPU guide](https://sageinfinity.github.io/docs/Guides/DualGPUGuide)).

Some paths are closed on the evidence. Diffusion depth and learned G-buffer estimators are orders of magnitude over budget. NVDS made Depth Anything V2 worse in an independent test. INT8 ran slower than FP16 on a vision transformer, and token pruning gained almost nothing on DPT-style heads. A flow network beside the depth network doubles the scheduling problem for no proven gain. D3D12 video motion estimation returns encoder blocks from NV12 input with unclear driver support. DLSS Super Resolution on captured frames cannot add detail without jitter. Heavy Gaussian blur and multi-pass dilation from the stereo tools would erase the detail that ambient occlusion reads. Matching flow on estimated depth would feed depth noise back into the motion meant to remove it. Raising process-wide GPU priority steals from the game, and a hang was reported at the realtime class with a D3D11 encoder ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nvenc-encoder-hangs-on-windows-when-using-d3d11-in-real-time-mode/357465)).

### Licence constraints that shape the list

Model weights divide cleanly. Apache-2.0 covers Depth Anything V2 Small, Depth Anything 3 Small, Base, Mono-Large and Metric-Large, and Video Depth Anything Small, so Unishade can offer them for download. Depth Anything V2 Base and Large, the larger Video Depth Anything models, Depth Anything 3 Large and Giant, oVDA, VeloDepth and the game-tuned Ultimate DAV2 student are non-commercial, and Depth Pro 2 is research-only. An MIT application permits commercial use downstream, so none of these belongs in the default catalogue. iw3 handles the same problem by requiring manual download of non-commercial checkpoints ([iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)). Distill-Any-Depth and MoGe weights have no stated licence and need the authors' confirmation. The game-depth datasets with real z-buffers are non-commercial or research-only, which permits evaluation and rules out shipping weights or data derived from them without checking. Training data for a Unishade student should be Unishade's own captures.

Code follows the same split. FidelityFX Optical Flow, XeGTAO, FSR1, Glamarye and iw3 are MIT, CShade is BSD-3-Clause, and ReShade.fxh is CC0. The ReShade motion shaders, qUINT MXAO, iMMERSE and SuperDepth3D cannot be copied. Their algorithms are free to reimplement.

Runtimes are downloadable on demand with care about the source. ONNX Runtime is MIT. The TensorRT for RTX agreement allows distribution only as incorporated into an application and forbids any use that would subject the software to an open-source licence ([NVIDIA licence agreement](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/tensorrt-rtx/latest/reference/sla.html)). Fetching it from NVIDIA's own distribution point at the user's request and keeping it out of the repository looks compatible, re-hosting it is distribution under those terms, and the project should confirm before shipping. This is a reading of the text and is not legal advice. The NVIDIA Optical Flow SDK carries the same open-source clause, which is the reason to prefer the Vulkan extension.

The DLSS 5 DLL is the hard case. `nvngx_dlssnr.dll` is not in the public DLSS SDK, whose releases contain no DLSS 5 header, document or note ([NVIDIA/DLSS releases](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/releases)), so the SDK's redistribution grant does not reach it. Version 310.8.0.0 first appeared publicly inside an early-access NBA 2K27 build ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/113305/nvidias-dlss-5-neural-rendering-tech-has-been-found-in-the-first-publicly-accessible-game/index.html)). The SDK licence also forbids bypassing any technical limitation ([DLSS SDK licence](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)), and the community builds that run on RTX 20 to 40 cards do exactly that and lose NVIDIA's signature ([DLSS 5 Video Player third-party notes](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/THIRD_PARTY.md)). The lowest-risk posture is the one video2dlssnr takes, which ships no NVIDIA DLLs and tells users that obtaining the runtime is their responsibility under NVIDIA's terms ([video2dlssnr](https://github.com/DaniilSokolyuk/video2dlssnr)). For Unishade that means the on-demand downloader should not fetch this DLL. The add-on should load an unpatched, signed copy from a path the user supplies, state plainly that the feature is unofficial, and accept that this limits it to RTX 50 hardware.

## Conclusion

The two add-ons turn out to need one shared module. A motion field with a confidence mask, a cut detector and a HUD mask stabilizes depth for ReShade shaders and is also the input DLSS 5 is sensitive to, while depth itself earns its place through ambient occlusion and depth of field and hardly at all through DLSS 5. Estimated depth may still help DLSS 5 indirectly as one of the tests that reject bad flow vectors, the role real depth plays in DLSS5-Feeder, though nobody has tried that with estimated depth. This inverts the framing of depth as the route to DLSS 5. The route is motion, and depth work should be judged by what shaders render from it.

Unishade's position is better than the gaps suggest. Nobody has published DirectML timings for this model, motion-compensated filtering of monocular depth, or a permissively licensed small model trained on real game depth, and Unishade can produce all three from a capture rig it needs anyway. The risk that engineering cannot remove is the DLSS 5 path itself. It rests on a reverse-engineered parameter list and a DLL with no public licence, and it becomes ordinary integration work only if NVIDIA publishes the header that its own Remix runtime already includes.

# Depth refinement for shading: turning low-res relative depth into a shader-usable depth buffer

Notes as of 2026-11-24. "Cited findings" are things I read in the linked source during this session. "Inferences" are my own derivations and cost estimates and are not from a source. Where I only saw a search-engine summary of a page and could not open the page itself, the bullet says so.

Reading conventions used below: `D` is disparity (inverse depth), `z` is view distance, `F` is ReShade's far plane (1000 by default), `p` is a full-res pixel, `q` is a low-res (model) pixel.

## 1. Guided upsampling and edge alignment

### Takeaway
No single classic filter gives both crisp silhouettes and clean interiors. Game engines solve half-res upsampling with a two-branch scheme: plain interpolation where the 2x2 or 3x3 low-res depths agree, and a pick-one-sample or depth-weighted rule at discontinuities. For Unishade the analogous design is bilinear or bicubic on smooth regions, with no colour involved, and a colour-selected foreground or background snap on edge pixels. The guided filter is good inside surfaces and bad at silhouettes, where it halos.

### Cited findings

Joint bilateral upsampling and relatives
- Joint bilateral upsampling comes from Kopf, Cohen, Lischinski and Uyttendaele, SIGGRAPH 2007. A solution computed on a downsampled image is upsampled with the high-res image as the prior. The paper lists tone mapping, colorization, stereo depth and photomontage as applications. Source: [JBU project page](https://johanneskopf.de/publications/jbu/)
- A later feature-upsampling paper describes JBU as training-free but limited "by its fixed, hand-crafted kernel design". Source: [Upsample Anything, arXiv 2511.16301](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16301v2)
- Riemens et al., SPIE 2009, upsample depth in multiple stages, refining 2x2 per stage, instead of one large-factor JBU. I saw the search summary only. Source: [Multistep joint bilateral depth upsampling](https://proceedings.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/7257/72570M/Multistep-joint-bilateral-depth-upsampling/10.1117/12.805640.full)

Guided filter and local linear models
- Bart Wronski's write-up fits a local linear model between guide `x` and signal `y` from four accumulated moments `X=Σx, Y=Σy, X2=Σx², XY=Σxy`, with `A = (XY − X·Y) / (X2 − X·X + reg)` and `B = Y − A·X`, moments normalised by the weight sum. The model is computed at low resolution, the coefficients `A, B` are interpolated with hardware bilinear, and the full-res result is one multiply-add, `y_hi = A·x_hi + B`. Source: [Local linear models and guided filtering](https://bartwronski.com/2019/09/22/local-linear-models-guided-filter/)
- The same article lists the failure cases. Smudging and halos appear near strong discontinuities because large jumps dominate the local fit. Holes in the low-res signal extrapolate badly. A flat or uncorrelated guide makes the fit ill-posed, so the regulariser pushes the result toward a local average. Memory is at least twice that of a bilateral filter and squared moments need more precision. It gives no recommended value for the regulariser. Source: [Local linear models and guided filtering](https://bartwronski.com/2019/09/22/local-linear-models-guided-filter/)
- The fast guided filter computes the filter at a subsampled resolution, which cuts cost from O(N) to O(N/s²) for subsampling ratio `s`, with a claimed speedup above 10x "with almost no visible degradation". Source: [He and Sun, arXiv 1505.00996](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.00996)

Bilateral solver, weighted mode
- The fast bilateral solver is an edge-aware optimiser that the authors report as 10 to 1000 times faster than competing optimisation approaches, with depth super-resolution among its four test tasks. The abstract gives no absolute runtime. Source: [Barron and Poole, arXiv 1511.03296](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03296)
- Weighted mode filtering builds a joint histogram of depth candidates weighted by colour similarity and takes the global mode. The authors state it is optimal in the L1 sense and apply it to raising resolution and suppressing noise of depth video with a high-res colour guide. I saw the publication abstract via search. Source: [Min, Lu, Do, IEEE TIP 2012](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/depth-video-enhancement-based-on-weighted-mode-filtering/)

What game engines do for half-res effects
- NVIDIA's nearest-depth filter, from Jansen and Bavoil's 2011 white paper on opacity-mapped particles, fetches the 2x2 low-res depths around each output pixel and uses one low-res colour sample instead of a weighted average. The paper says this is more robust and faster. The Order: 1886 used a variant. I could not parse the PDF text, so this is from the search summary. Source: [NVIDIA OpacityMapping white paper](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/assets/gamedev/files/sdk/11/OpacityMappingSDKWhitePaper.pdf)
- Mikkel Gjoel's fog upsampler for INSIDE stores the background depth next to each low-res texel. It takes four low-res taps on a blue-noise rotated disc of radius 3.1 low-res texels. If `(max − min) / avg < 0.1` over the four stored depths it averages them and never reads full-res depth. Otherwise it weights each tap by `w_i = 1 / (|d_i − d_full| + 1e-5)`. He notes the weights are a soft fall-off, so bad texels still contribute a little. Source: [Depth-aware upsampling, pixelmager](https://pixelmager.github.io/depth_aware_upsampling/upsampling)
- A second write-up makes the same point that full-res depth only needs sampling near depth edges, and warns that depth-aware weighting leaves TAA with too few distinct neighbourhood values. Search summary only. Source: [tcantenot gist](https://gist.github.com/tcantenot/15b5dc4056ddc07540a1379745edbf40)
- XeGTAO builds its depth mip chain with a weighted average of the four samples, weighted by whether each depth is within a threshold of the most distant sample. Its denoiser is a 5x5 depth-aware spatial filter that uses edge information written by the main pass. The whole three-pass effect costs about 1.4 ms at 3840x2160 on an RTX 3070 and 0.56 ms at 1920x1080 on an RTX 2060 at the High preset. Licence MIT. Intel has discontinued the project. Source: [XeGTAO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO/master/README.md)
- qUINT MXAO's blur and upsample weights each tap by `saturate(1 − |Δdepth|) · saturate(dot(n_tap, n_center)·16 − 15)` and falls back to an unweighted average when the total weight is below 2. It runs 4 steps at the render scale, then 8 steps to reach full resolution. Source: [qUINT_mxao.fx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/martymcmodding/qUINT/master/Shaders/qUINT_mxao.fx)

FSR1 EASU
- EASU is a locally adaptive elliptical Lanczos-like filter spanning ±2 taps with a strong negative lobe. AMD says its input should be well anti-aliased, normalised to 0 to 1, and in perceptual colour space. Community ports expose a deringing switch. FSR1 is MIT. I found no source that applies EASU to a depth buffer. Sources: [GPUOpen FSR1](https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-superresolution/), [FidelityFX SDK manual](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-spatial/)

Flying pixels and what 2D-to-3D tools do
- Discriminative depth models such as Depth Anything v2 smooth edges and predict intermediate values between foreground and background, which become flying pixels in 3D. An edge-aware Chamfer metric gives Depth Anything v2 0.18, Depth Pro 0.14, MoGe-2 0.13 and Pixel-Perfect Depth 0.07, lower is better. The search summary warned that the table columns were garbled, so check the numbers before quoting them. Source: [Pixel-Perfect Visual Geometry Estimation, arXiv 2601.05246](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05246)
- A 2026 Jetson benchmark measures "flying edge points", predicted edge pixels that land in front of the nearest or behind the farthest ground-truth surface. Depth Anything V2-S scores 25.8%, V2-L 20.3%, DA3 Metric-L 16.6%, UniDepth V2-L 17.1%, MoGe-2-L 46.1%. Stereo methods score 1 to 9%. The paper notes that AbsRel barely registers this error. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)
- iw3, the 2D-to-3D converter in nunif, has an "Edge Fix" option, `--edge-dilation`, which "reduces artifacts by dilating foreground segments (high value area)". Default is 2. The README says 4 is "the most eye-friendly, but it degrades depth accuracy". Separate X and Y values are supported. Source: [iw3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nagadomi/nunif/master/iw3/README.md)
- The iw3 author reports that Depth Anything V2 and Video Depth Anything outputs have jagged edges, more visible once resized to input resolution, and suspects training depth that was downscaled with nearest neighbour. He added `--depth-aa` as an anti-aliasing pass, noted that Distill Any Depth output is already anti-aliased, and later found the difference hard to see once Edge Fix was on. Source: [nunif issue 406](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/406)
- Robust Video Matting reports 4K at 76 FPS and HD at 104 FPS on a GTX 1080 Ti. Source: [arXiv 2108.11515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11515). The deep guided filter is a guided filtering layer that takes a low-res network output plus a high-res guidance map and produces a high-res output, trainable end to end. Source: [Wu et al., arXiv 1803.05619](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05619)

### Inferences
- The JBU formula in its usual form, written from memory because the paper PDF exceeded the fetch size limit, is `S(p) = (1/k_p) Σ_q S_lo(q) · f(|p/s − q|) · g(|I(p) − I_hi(q·s)|)` with `f` a spatial Gaussian over low-res coordinates and `g` a range Gaussian on the guide. Unishade's current filter is this with spatial sigma 1 model pixel and colour sigma 0.1.
- Texture copy in JBU has a specific cause. On a surface of constant depth the normalised weights cannot change the result. On a sloped surface, a colour edge skews the weights to one side, which moves the weighted centroid of the taps, so the output takes the depth of a point up to a model pixel away. A textured floor then gets depth ripples that follow the texture, and finite-difference normals turn those ripples into embossing. Two cheap fixes follow. First, do not use colour at all where the low-res neighbourhood has no discontinuity. Second, extrapolate each tap to the output position with the low-res gradient before weighting, `d_q + ∇d_q · (p − q)`, so a skewed centroid no longer changes the value on a plane.
- A sketch that combines the engine practice above with weighted mode filtering, sized for the 1 ms budget:
  1. Per output pixel, gather the 3x3 low-res disparities. Compute `lo`, `hi`.
  2. If `(hi − lo) / hi < τ`, with τ around 0.03 to 0.1 as in Gjoel's 0.1 test, output bilinear or Catmull-Rom of disparity. No colour taps, so no texture copy.
  3. Otherwise split the nine taps into a near and a far cluster at `(lo + hi) / 2`. Compare the full-res pixel colour with the mean low-res colour of each cluster and take the closer cluster. Output the spatially weighted mean of that cluster only. This is weighted mode filtering with two bins, and it removes intermediate values, so there are no flying pixels and the silhouette is one pixel wide and follows the colour edge.
  4. Optional foreground dilation by half to one model pixel, as iw3 does, hides the background halo that models leave around thin foreground objects. It also thickens every object, which is wrong for AO contact shadows, so keep it small.
- Estimated cost, mine. Steps 1 and 2 are nine to sixteen depth taps per pixel, which is four gather calls. Step 3 runs on the few percent of pixels near silhouettes and adds nine depth and nine colour taps there, if the downsampled colour frame the model consumed is kept as a texture. XeGTAO's full three-pass effect with far more samples runs in 1.4 ms at 4K on an RTX 3070, so a single pass like this should land around 0.1 to 0.3 ms at 1440p on the same class of GPU. Measure it.
- The guided filter is the wrong tool for the silhouette step, given Wronski's halo finding, but its linear model is a good smoother inside a surface if the regression is over screen position instead of colour, which is plane fitting. See section 5.
- EASU applied to depth would overshoot at edges because of its negative lobe and produce depths outside the local range. It also analyses the image it upsamples, so it sharpens edges where the low-res depth has them and does not align them to the colour frame. I would not use it for depth.
- Interpolate in disparity or in the stored ReShade depth value, not in linear distance. A plane is linear in screen space in `1/z`, and ReShade's stored value is affine in `1/z` (section 3), so bilinear interpolation there is exact on planes.
- The iw3 remark about jagged Depth Anything V2 edges versus anti-aliased Distill Any Depth edges suggests that swapping the checkpoint could change edge quality at no runtime cost. Whether a small Distill Any Depth checkpoint exists and how it behaves on games is unverified.

### Gaps
- I could not read the exact nearest-depth edge test in the NVIDIA white paper. From memory the SDK sample falls back to bilinear when all four low-res depths are within a threshold of the full-res depth. Confirm against the sample source.
- Not re-read this session and so uncited: the bilateral grid of Chen, Paris and Durand 2007, bilateral guided upsampling of Chen et al. 2016, HDRNet, and constant-time weighted median filtering of Ma et al. 2013. A bilateral grid is attractive in principle because slicing is one trilinear fetch per pixel, but I have no measured GPU numbers for depth.
- I found no compute-shader implementation of a fast guided filter or bilateral solver with a clear licence and published timings.
- I did not confirm from a primary source that Robust Video Matting uses the deep guided filter for its high-res path. I believe it does.
- No source compares these filters on monocular depth feeding AO or GI. All parameter suggestions above need testing on captured game frames.

## 2. Learned upsamplers and refiners that are cheap

### Takeaway
Nothing I found runs in 1 to 2 ms at 1080p. Feature upsamplers cost 10 to 200 ms at 448x448 output and run out of memory near 1024x1024. Depth refiners such as SharpDepth and Depth Pro are tens to hundreds of times over budget. The only learned options that fit are layers baked into the depth model itself.

### Cited findings
- AnyUp is an attention-based feature upsampler that works with any encoder. At 448x448 output it takes about 20.6 ms with 0.8M parameters. JAFAR takes 26.8 ms, LoftUp 204.1 ms, FeatUp's JBU variant 10.4 ms. AnyUp's depth and normal results come from linear probes on upsampled DINOv2 features. It does not upsample depth maps. Source: [AnyUp, arXiv 2510.12764](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12764v2)
- A second table, for 16x upsampling of a 384x28x28 feature map, gives LoftUp 4 FPS, AnyUp 5 FPS, JAFAR 11 FPS, FeatUp 17 FPS and NAF 18 FPS. Search summary only, and the summary flagged the JBU row as anomalous. Source: [NAF, arXiv 2511.18452](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18452v1)
- Upsample Anything fits per-pixel anisotropic Gaussian kernels at test time and does apply to raw depth maps with an RGB guide. It takes about 0.419 s per 224x224 image, though one of its tables says 0.0419 s. Its peak memory reaches about 82 GB at 1024x1024, and AnyUp is out of memory above 512x512 in the same table. Its own NYUv2 depth-map numbers are inconsistent, with RMSE worse than bilinear in one table. Source: [arXiv 2511.16301](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16301v2)
- Depth Pro has 504M parameters and takes 341 ms on a V100 for its fixed 1536x1536 input. Its sharp boundaries come from a second training stage on synthetic data only, with first and second derivative losses, Scharr gradient, Laplace and squared gradient. For real datasets it discards the top 20% of per-pixel errors because real boundary labels are noisy. Source: [Depth Pro, arXiv 2410.02073](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02073v1). On a Jetson AGX Orin it takes 802.5 ms. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)
- SharpDepth is a diffusion-based refiner for metric depth estimators such as UniDepth, trained without ground-truth depth through score distillation. Search summary only. Source: [SharpDepth, arXiv 2411.18229](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.18229v1)
- FDSR, a guided depth super-resolution baseline from CVPR 2021, reports 0.007 s per frame against 0.140 s for DKN. The search summary did not give the resolution. Source: [arXiv 2104.06174](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.06174)
- Lightweight whole models keep appearing. ZipDepth, 2026, is described as a 6.1M-parameter zero-shot model reaching 34 FPS in PyTorch FP32 and up to 77 FPS in TensorRT FP16 on a 15 W Jetson Orin NX. BoRe-Depth, 2025, puts boundary refinement inside a small self-supervised model for embedded targets. Search summaries only. Sources: [ZipDepth](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.08771.md), [BoRe-Depth, arXiv 2511.04388](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04388v1)
- Running a model away from its training resolution can hurt more than it helps. Depth Anything 3 Metric-L goes from 12.3% AbsRel at its native 350x504 input to 14.2% at 700x1008 and 54.9% at full resolution. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)

### Inferences
- A rough cost floor for any CNN at frame resolution, my arithmetic. One 3x3 convolution with 16 input and 16 output channels at 1280x720 is about 2.1 GFLOPs. A three-layer guidance network is 5 to 7 GFLOPs per frame, and through an ONNX runtime with tensor copies that is several milliseconds on a mid-range GPU. So a learned upsampler has to run at model resolution and emit something a compute shader can apply at full resolution.
- Two designs fit that constraint. The first is a guided-filter-style head. The model emits coefficient maps at low resolution, the shader bilinearly samples them and applies them to the full-res guide, exactly the low-res-fit, full-res-apply structure Wronski describes. The second is a convex-upsampling head as in RAFT, where the model predicts per-pixel weights over the 3x3 coarse neighbours for each subpixel and the shader does the weighted sum. Both need training data with sharp ground-truth depth, which the game-capture datasets in section 7 can supply. Neither exists off the shelf for Depth Anything V2 Small as far as I found.
- Spending the same budget on a larger model input is the more reliable lever. The benchmark numbers above say edges and thin structures are limited by what the model resolves, and no post-filter recovers a fence the model never saw.

### Gaps
- I did not verify RAFT's convex upsampling details from the paper this session. The abstract page has none. Pointer: [arXiv 2003.12039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12039).
- I found no 2025 or 2026 guided depth super-resolution paper with a stated per-frame time at 1080p.
- LiFT, FeatSharp, PatchRefiner and BoostingMonocularDepth were not checked. The first two are feature upsamplers of the same cost class as AnyUp. The last two are tile-based multi-pass methods and far too slow by design.
- No published timing exists for a refinement head appended to Depth Anything V2 Small in ONNX.

## 3. Mapping relative inverse depth to linear depth

### Takeaway
A global scale error is harmless to geometry and only changes effect radii and distances. A shift error is what distorts shape. For a model that predicts disparity, an unknown disparity shift is a projective remap of depth. It keeps planes flat but changes the angles between them and compresses or stretches the far range. Peak normalisation should be replaced by a robust percentile with temporal smoothing and scene-cut reset, which is what iw3 does.

### Cited findings

Why shift matters
- "An unknown scale in depth will not cause any shape distortion, as it scales the 3D scene uniformly", while "a shift of d will affect the x, y, and z coordinates non-uniformly". The distortions "are immediately recognizable when viewing the point cloud at an oblique angle" but "cannot be observed looking at a depth map alone". Focal length "scales x and y coordinates, but not z". The authors recover shift and focal length with two point-cloud networks, starting from an assumed 60 degree field of view. Source: [Yin et al., Learning to Recover 3D Scene Shape from a Single Image, arXiv 2012.09365](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.09365)
- Depth Anything trains on disparity. Depth is "transformed into the disparity space by d=1/t and then normalized to 0∼1 on each depth map". The loss aligns prediction and label by subtracting the median and dividing by the mean absolute deviation "to ignore the unknown scale and shift of each sample". Sky is found with a segmentation model and the pipeline will "set its disparity value as 0 (farthest)". Source: [Depth Anything, arXiv 2401.10891](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10891v1)
- MoGe evaluates affine-invariant disparity by least-squares fitting a scale and an offset in disparity space. MoGe itself predicts an affine-invariant point map with unknown global scale and 3D shift, and MoGe-2 adds metric scale. Search summary of the papers. Sources: [MoGe, arXiv 2410.19115](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19115v3), [MoGe-2, arXiv 2507.02546](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02546v1)

Models that remove the ambiguity
- Depth Pro predicts a canonical inverse depth `C` and recovers metric depth as `D_m = f_px / (w · C)`, with `f_px` the focal length in pixels and `w` the image width. A separate small head predicts horizontal field of view. Source: [Depth Pro](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02073v1)
- Depth Anything 3 has a metric model whose output converts with `metric_depth = focal * net_output / 300`, focal in pixels. DA3Mono-Large "directly predicts depth" instead of disparity. Both are ViT-L at 0.35B parameters and Apache 2.0, and both have a sky segmentation output. DA3-Small, 0.08B, and DA3-Base are Apache 2.0 but have neither metric depth nor sky output. DA3-Large and Giant are CC BY-NC 4.0. Source: [Depth Anything 3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3/main/README.md)
- Depth Anything V2 metric models are fine-tuned on Hypersim for indoor with max depth 20 and on Virtual KITTI 2 for outdoor with max depth 80. They exist at vits 24.8M, vitb and vitl sizes and output depth in metres. Source: [DAv2 metric_depth README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DepthAnything/Depth-Anything-V2/main/metric_depth/README.md)
- MoGe-2 has a ViT-S checkpoint with metric scale and normals at 35M parameters, plus ViT-B at 104M and ViT-L at 331M. MoGe-3 was released on 2026-08-18 with ViT-L and ViT-G checkpoints. The README quotes 60 ms per image for ViT-L in FP16 on an A100 or RTX 3090. Code is MIT, the bundled DINOv2 code is Apache 2.0, and the README states no licence for the weights. ONNX export is documented. Source: [MoGe README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/MoGe/main/README.md)
- Jetson AGX Orin, TensorRT FP16, median GPU time. Depth Anything V2-S 11.5 ms at 518x770. DA3 Metric-L 33.2 ms at 350x504. UniDepth V2-S 42.5 ms at 644x938. Metric3D v2-S 54.5 ms at 616x1064. MoGe-2-S 72.6 ms at 700x1008. Raw metric AbsRel on Middlebury is 12.3% for DA3 Metric-L and 12.0% for Depth Pro, and 20 to 60% for the others. With an oracle per-image scale it is 3.8 to 8.9% for all of them, and Depth Anything V2-S is the worst at 8.9%. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)

Normalisation over time
- iw3 stabilises the depth range with an exponential moving average of the min and max, `--ema-normalize`. The appropriate decay "is between 0.75 and 0.99", and too large a value clips when the range changes suddenly. It detects shot boundaries with TransNetV2 and resets the normalisation state there. It notes that Video Depth Anything originally normalises with a global min and max, which a streaming tool cannot do. It offers both a "Max Scaler" and a "Min-Max Scaler" variant of DA3 Mono. Source: [iw3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nagadomi/nunif/master/iw3/README.md)
- iw3 clips Depth Pro distance at 40 m. Source: [iw3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nagadomi/nunif/master/iw3/README.md)

What ReShade shaders assume
- ReShade.fxh, licence CC0-1.0, defaults to `RESHADE_DEPTH_LINEARIZATION_FAR_PLANE 1000.0`, `RESHADE_DEPTH_INPUT_IS_REVERSED 1`, `IS_LOGARITHMIC 0`, `RESHADE_DEPTH_MULTIPLIER 1`. `GetLinearizedDepth` reads the buffer, applies `depth = 1 − depth` if reversed, then with `N = 1` computes `depth /= FAR − depth * (FAR − N)`. Source: [ReShade.fxh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/crosire/reshade-shaders/slim/Shaders/ReShade.fxh)
- qUINT MXAO reconstructs position as `(uv.xyx * float3(2,2,0) + float3(-1,-1,1)) * linear_depth(uv) * RESHADE_DEPTH_LINEARIZATION_FAR_PLANE`. No field of view or aspect ratio is applied. A variant with a 75 degree vertical FOV exists but sits inside `#if 0`. Its sample radius is `0.25 * MXAO_SAMPLE_RADIUS / (samples * (scaled_depth + 2.0))`, so the screen-space radius shrinks as depth grows. Source: [qUINT_mxao.fx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/martymcmodding/qUINT/master/Shaders/qUINT_mxao.fx)
- XeGTAO takes an effect radius in world units and a falloff range, converts depth to view space in its first pass, and warns that the depth buffer is treated as a heightfield, so thin features at discontinuities cast excess occlusion and haloing grows with radius. Source: [XeGTAO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO/master/README.md)
- ReShade users tune the reversed and logarithmic flags by trial until surface normals look smooth in a depth-display shader. Search summary of forum posts. Source: [reshade.me depth buffer thread](https://reshade.me/forum/general-discussion/4083-depth-buffer-detection-modifications?start=900)

### Inferences
- ReShade's linearisation, worked through. Substituting the standard projection `d = F(z − N) / (z(F − N))` into `d / (F − d(F − N))` gives `(z − N) / (N(F − N))`. With `N = 1` the function returns `L = (z − 1) / (F − 1)`. So the buffer value a shader expects is `d = F·L / (1 + L(F − 1))`, stored as `1 − d` under the default reversed flag, and `d = F/(F−1) · (1 − 1/z)` is affine in `1/z`. Writing Unishade's `z = Nearest / closeness` into that gives a stored value that is affine in the model's disparity. Interpolating the stored value is then perspective-correct on planes. Check that the add-on's mapping matches this exactly, since using `L = z/F` instead of `(z − 1)/(F − 1)` breaks the affine relation slightly.
- The effect of a disparity shift, my derivation. Let the model output be `m = a·(1/z) + b`. Mapping `z' = k / m` gives `z' = (k/a) · z / (1 + s·z)` with `s = b/a`. In homogeneous coordinates the reconstructed point is `(X, Y, Z, (1 + s·Z)·a/k)`, which is a 3D projective transform of the true point. Planes map to planes, so flat walls stay flat and AO on them stays clean. Angles are not preserved. With `s > 0` depth saturates at `k/(a·s)`, far surfaces flatten toward a backdrop, and a receding floor tilts up toward the camera. With `s < 0` depth diverges at `z = −1/s` and everything beyond is pushed to the far plane. This differs from the Yin et al. case of a shift added to depth, where `1/(z + t)` is no longer linear in screen space and planes do bend.
- Depth Anything's training labels put the farthest point of each image at disparity 0 and sky at 0. If the network inherits that habit, an indoor scene with no sky has its farthest wall mapped to infinity by `Nearest / closeness`, which stretches the back of every room. Whether Depth Anything V2 Small does this on game frames is an empirical question. Fit `(a, b)` against real game depth and look at the distribution of `b/a`. If it is consistently non-zero, a constant correction or a per-frame estimate is worth adding.
- Scale errors do not change normals in MXAO-style reconstruction, because `P = (ndc.x·z, ndc.y·z, z)` scales uniformly with `z`. They do change the AO radius, which MXAO ties to depth, plus fog density, DoF focus distance and ray-march step lengths. So flicker in the peak estimate shows up as pulsing AO radius and pumping fog, not as shimmering normals.
- MXAO's reconstruction implies `tan(half FOV) = 1` on both axes with no aspect correction. Other shaders assume other values. Since focal length scales x and y but not z, the "right" depth scale for geometrically plausible normals depends on the consuming shader's assumed FOV. A user-facing "depth strength" control that scales distance relative to the near anchor is the practical answer, and it is the same knob as iw3's foreground scale.
- Replace the single peak with a high percentile. One hot pixel on a weapon tip, a HUD icon or a particle currently sets the scale for the whole frame. A 256-bin histogram of the low-res output, which is 50k to 400k pixels, gives the 99th to 99.9th percentile for the near anchor and a low percentile for a far anchor. Smooth both with an EMA in iw3's 0.75 to 0.99 range and reset on scene cuts. TransNetV2 is too heavy here. A cheap cut detector is a large jump in the frame's luma histogram or in the percentile estimates themselves.
- A metric or scale-invariant model would remove the shift problem at the source. The realistic candidates by licence and size are Depth Anything V2 metric vits, MoGe-2 ViT-S and UniDepth V2-S. The first shares its architecture with Depth Anything V2-S, so I assume the same cost. On the Orin the other two take about 6x and about 4x the time of Depth Anything V2-S, though at different input sizes, so the ratios are not like for like. The oracle-scale numbers say MoGe-2-S and UniDepth V2-S have much better shape, 4.9% and 5.7% against 8.9%, which is the property that matters for normals. The Virtual KITTI 2 fine-tune of Depth Anything V2 is trained on synthetic driving, which is closer to game imagery than most real datasets. All untested on games.
- For sky and "infinitely far", clamp to the far plane so the linearised value is exactly 1, and keep everything else strictly below 1. ReShade shaders commonly test for depth 1 to skip sky (section 6).

### Gaps
- I found no measurement of the residual shift of Depth Anything V2 on game content.
- I did not verify how iMMERSE, RTGI or other current Pascal Gilcher shaders handle FOV. I believe they expose an FOV setting. Their licence forbids redistribution, so they can only be tested, not bundled.
- The licence of Depth Anything V2 Small was not confirmed this session. The iw3 README says the Base and Large variants are CC BY-NC 4.0 and does not list Small among them, which matches my memory that Small is Apache 2.0.
- No source gives per-frame cost for FOV estimation on game frames. Depth Pro's FOV head rides on a 504M-parameter model.

## 4. Normals

### Takeaway
On a depth buffer with pixel-sharp discontinuities, the 5-tap "accurate" reconstruction is the best finite-difference scheme and costs 9 depth taps. It assumes one-pixel-wide edges, so it only helps after the upsampler has snapped silhouettes. Predicted normals are available from MoGe-2 and MoGe-3 but cost several times a Depth Anything V2-S pass, and third-party ReShade shaders derive their own normals from depth anyway, so a predicted normal map mainly helps as a guide for refining depth.

### Cited findings
- Ben Golus's Unity shader lists four variants with compiled costs. 3-tap, 41 math and 3 texture ops, has "artifacts on depth disparities". 4-tap, 50 math and 4 tex, softens sharp details and has worse artifacts on depth disparities. The improved method after János Turánszki, 62 math and 5 tex, picks the horizontal and the vertical neighbour with the smaller z difference and has "worse artifacts on convex edges than either 3 tap or 4 tap". The accurate method after Yuwen Wu, 66 math and 9 tex, has "no artifacts on depth disparities" and "no artifacts on edges" but "artifacts on triangles that are <3 pixels across". The gist states no licence. Source: [bgolus gist](https://gist.github.com/bgolus/a07ed65602c009d5e2f753826e8078a0)
- Yuwen Wu's method takes two taps on each side along each axis. For each side it extrapolates the two taps to the centre and keeps the side whose extrapolation is closer to the centre depth. The extrapolation is linear in inverse depth, which is perspective-correct. With `x` the nearer tap and `z` the farther, the extrapolated depth is `x·z / (2z − x)`. The post explains why three taps fail. The nearer neighbour by depth can sit on the wrong surface. The site content is CC BY-NC 4.0 and the code has no separate licence. Ben Golus's Unity version works on non-linear depth, which changes the extrapolation to a plain linear one. Source: [Accurate Normal Reconstruction from Depth Buffer](https://atyuwen.github.io/posts/normal-reconstruction)
- Turánszki's idea is to find the normal of the triangle the pixel belongs to. Both URLs for his post returned 404 when I fetched them, so this is from the search summary. Source: [Wicked Engine blog](https://wickedengine.net/2019/09/improved-normal-reconstruction-from-depth)
- qUINT MXAO uses the Turánszki-style rule, keeping the position delta with the smaller `|Δz|` on each axis, then `normalize(cross(dy, dx))`. It has an optional normal smoothing pass, off by default, that samples four directions with up to five exponentially spaced steps and weights neighbours by position distance and normal angle. Source: [qUINT_mxao.fx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/martymcmodding/qUINT/master/Shaders/qUINT_mxao.fx)
- XeGTAO recommends supplying screen-space normals and falls back to a built-in depth-to-normal generator. Bent normals add about 25% cost. Source: [XeGTAO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO/master/README.md)
- Glamarye's Fast AO uses no normals at all. It samples a circle around each pixel and counts samples closer to the camera. Licence MIT. Source: [Glamarye README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rj200/Glamarye_Fast_Effects_for_ReShade/main/README.md)
- MoGe-2 and MoGe-3 checkpoints output normals alongside metric points. The smallest normal-capable one is `moge-2-vits-normal` at 35M parameters. Source: [MoGe README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/MoGe/main/README.md)
- Depth Anything 3 documents no normal output. Its listed outputs are depth, confidence, extrinsics and intrinsics. Source: [Depth Anything 3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3/main/README.md)
- Diffusion-based normal estimators are the other family. StableNormal's one-step estimator is reported as on par with DSINE in accuracy. Lotus claims to be hundreds of times faster than Marigold and slightly faster than Depth Anything V2 at high resolution. DSINE is a feed-forward regressor. NormalCrafter targets temporally consistent normals for video. I found no millisecond figures for any of them. Search summaries only. Sources: [StableNormal, arXiv 2406.16864](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16864v1), [Lotus, arXiv 2409.18124](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.18124v5), [NormalCrafter, arXiv 2504.11427](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11427v1)

### Inferences
- The side-selection methods assume a discontinuity is one pixel wide. Depth upsampled from 1/2 to 1/8 resolution with a smooth filter has ramps several pixels wide at silhouettes, so both the Turánszki rule and the 5-tap rule pick a side that is still on the ramp, and the normal points sideways along the whole silhouette band. This is the "soft edges" artefact in AO. The fix is upstream. Snap the ramp to one pixel, as in section 1, and the shaders' existing reconstruction starts working.
- Because Unishade writes the stored non-linear depth, which is affine in `1/z`, a shader can run Wu's test with plain linear extrapolation, `2·d1 − d2`, on the raw buffer value. That is the cheaper Golus form.
- Would predicted normals beat differentiated depth for AO and GI? For the lighting term, yes in principle. A network normal is not hurt by depth quantisation or upsampling ripples. Three things limit the benefit. MXAO, RTGI and most ReShade effects compute normals internally from depth and have no input for an external normal map, so only Unishade's own effects could use it. Horizon-based AO still marches the depth buffer, so its occlusion term inherits every depth error regardless of the normal. And the smallest normal-predicting model measured, MoGe-2-S, costs about 6x Depth Anything V2-S on the Orin at a larger input.
- The better use of a predicted normal map is as a constraint on depth. Either use it as an extra edge-stopping term in the upsampler and denoiser, the way SVGF and MXAO's blur use G-buffer normals, or nudge the depth gradient toward the predicted normal inside smooth regions. Both happen once in Unishade's pass and benefit every downstream shader.

### Gaps
- No real-time cost figures for DSINE, Metric3D v2 normals, Marigold normals or Lotus on consumer GPUs were found.
- I found no study comparing AO or GI quality from predicted normals against normals differentiated from predicted depth.
- Turánszki's original post could not be opened, so its code and stated cost are unverified here.

## 5. Quantization and banding

### Takeaway
Half-float precision is enough to break finite-difference normals on surfaces that face the camera. This applies both to an fp16 model output and to an R16F depth texture. Store depth in R32F, keep the model's last layers in fp32 if possible, and smooth within surfaces by plane fitting at model resolution before upsampling.

### Cited findings
- XeGTAO stores view-space depth in a 16-bit float buffer to save bandwidth and notes this "causes minor precision issues" and is incompatible with its built-in normal generator. Using fp16 shader math elsewhere gives a 5 to 20% speedup. Source: [XeGTAO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO/master/README.md)
- NVIDIA's scalable ambient obscurance paper argues that depth-buffer precision is key because every other quantity in the algorithm derives from it. Search summary only. Source: [McGuire et al., SAO](https://research.nvidia.com/sites/default/files/pubs/2012-06_Scalable-Ambient-Obscurance/McGuire12SAO.pdf)
- The Jetson benchmark found that native FP16 TensorRT engines of Depth Anything V2 agree with FP32 within 0.25%, and that some FP16 engines of other models were unreliable enough that it took their accuracy numbers from FP32 PyTorch. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)
- SVGF filters with a hierarchical image-space wavelet filter and runs in about 10 ms at 1920x1080 for the full denoiser. Source: [SVGF, NVIDIA Research](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2017-07_spatiotemporal-variance-guided-filtering-real-time-reconstruction-path-traced). A project write-up gives its depth edge-stopping weight as an exponential of `−|z_p − z_q| / (σ_z · |∇z · (p − q)| + ε)`, a test against the local depth plane that needs the screen-space depth gradient, and its normal weight as `max(0, n_p · n_q)^σ_n`. Search summary only. Source: [SVGF write-up](https://jacquespillet.github.io/SVGF)
- Gjoel adds triangular-PDF dither of `(2·rnd − 1)/255` when writing to an 8-bit target. Source: [pixelmager](https://pixelmager.github.io/depth_aware_upsampling/upsampling)
- Wronski notes that linear-model fitting needs more precision than the signal itself because it accumulates squared moments. Source: [Local linear models](https://bartwronski.com/2019/09/22/local-linear-models-guided-filter/)

### Inferences
All numbers in this block are my own arithmetic.
- A half float has 11 significant bits, so its relative step is between 2⁻¹¹ and 2⁻¹⁰, 0.05 to 0.1%. The step in disparity equals the relative step in distance. At 2560 pixels across a 90 degree field of view, one pixel subtends about 7.8e-4 in tangent units. A wall facing the camera should have near-zero depth change per pixel, so one quantisation step between adjacent pixels tilts the reconstructed normal by `atan(5e-4 / 7.8e-4)`, about 33 degrees, up to about 52 degrees for the larger step. Spread over four full-res pixels by the upsampler it is still 9 to 14 degrees. On a wall whose depth varies 1% across the screen there are only 10 to 20 levels, so the result is terraces 50 to 100 pixels wide with a visible crease at each step. This is the contour-line pattern in AO and normal debug views.
- The 0.25% FP16 agreement in the benchmark is an accuracy statement and says nothing about smoothness. A model can be accurate to 0.25% and still terrace.
- Fixes in order of preference. Keep the head's final convolution and activation in fp32 through mixed-precision export, so the output has 24 significant bits. If that is not possible, fit planes. At model resolution, for each pixel run a small weighted least-squares fit of disparity against screen position over a 5x5 or 7x7 window, with weights that reject samples far from the centre's local plane, the SVGF depth test. Planes are exact in disparity space, so this removes terraces on flat geometry without rounding corners more than the window size. At model resolution, 50k to 400k pixels, a 7x7 fit is under 20M taps and should cost well under 0.1 ms.
- An à-trous filter is the alternative when terraces are wider than the fit window. Three iterations with steps 1, 2, 4 at model resolution cover a 29-pixel footprint with 75 taps per pixel. Without G-buffer normals the only edge-stopping terms available are the plane-distance depth test and colour, and colour brings texture copy back, so use the depth test alone.
- Storage, assuming the mapping in section 3 with the nearest point at 10 units and the far plane at 1000. R16 UNORM of the stored value has steps of about 0.015% of distance at 10 units, 0.16% at 100 units, 0.76% at 500 and 1.5% at 1000, so the far half of the range bands. R16F with the default reversed convention stores a value close to `1/z − 1/F`, so its steps are 0.05 to 0.1% of distance over the whole range, the same as an fp16 model output and with the same terracing. R16F without reversal crams the scene into 0.9 to 1.0, where half floats step by 4.9e-4. That is 0.5% at 10 units, 5% at 100 and hundreds of units at the far end, which is unusable. R32F is safe either way, with a 24-bit mantissa. If a 16-bit format is forced, store disparity in UNORM16 and convert in the shader, which gives 0.0015% at the near anchor and 0.15% at the far plane.
- Dithering depth before differentiation is the wrong tool. Noise in depth becomes noise in normals at full amplitude. Dither only helps a consumer that integrates, such as fog.

### Gaps
- I did not read the SVGF paper's own equations or its default sigma values this session. The usual values quoted are σ_z = 1, σ_n = 128 and σ_l = 4. Confirm in the [KIT preprint](https://cg.ivd.kit.edu/publications/2017/svgf/svgf_preprint.pdf).
- No source measures terracing from fp16 monocular depth output. The estimate above needs a capture of a flat wall and a normal debug view to confirm.
- I did not check whether the ONNX runtimes Unishade uses on each platform support mixed fp16 and fp32 graphs without a speed penalty.

## 6. Problem content: HUD, sky, particles, transparency, thin structures

### Takeaway
Existing ReShade practice handles HUD and sky by convention and by hand, not by detection. Sky is "depth equals 1", menus are "depth equals 0", and HUD is a user-painted mask or a fixed-pixel colour probe. None of that transfers directly to captured frames, where the model sees the HUD as scene content. I found no documented automatic HUD detector for capture-only tools.

### Cited findings

HUD and UI
- UIMask uses a static user-made mask texture. The techniques `UIMask_Top` and `UIMask_Bottom` are placed at the top and bottom of the effect chain, a mask intensity controls the blend, and `UIMASK_MULTICHANNEL` allows three masks, one per colour channel. Search summary of forum posts. Source: [UIMask port thread](https://reshade.me/forum/shader-presentation/3006-i-ported-remade-uimask-for-3-0)
- UIDetect by brussell decides whether a given UI is on screen by checking user-defined pixel coordinates against expected RGB values, then toggles effects or applies an RGB mask texture. The author says pixel colours vary with resolution, anti-aliasing and possibly GPU vendor, so profiles do not transfer between setups. Semi-transparent UI defeats it. Cursor-based detection was unreliable across games. The thread states no licence. Source: [UIDetect thread](https://www.reshade.me/forum/shader-presentation/4024-uidetect)
- Glamarye's "Detect menus & videos" option skips all processing where the depth value is 0, per pixel. Its "Detect sky" option skips where depth is 1. It warns that both should only be enabled if depth is always available during gameplay. For games with their own background blur it has a mode that uses a blurred-area depth instead of per-pixel depth. Licence MIT. Source: [Glamarye README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rj200/Glamarye_Fast_Effects_for_ReShade/main/README.md)
- A forum user masking the HUD by depth compared depth to 0.0 and had to switch to 1.0 when the buffer was reversed. Some users report ReShade picking the HUD's depth buffer instead of the scene's. Search summary. Source: [reshade.me UI mask thread](https://reshade.me/forum/shader-presentation/5588-a-subtle-hack-ui-mask)
- The OmniWorld-Game dataset, captured with ReShade and OBS, removes UI elements and text "automatically via a ReShade plugin" and filters specific scenes by hand. Source: [OmniWorld, arXiv 2509.12201](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12201v1)
- Driver-level frame generation has the same blindness as Unishade. Reviews note ghosting around menus and HUD elements with AFMF and Lossless Scaling, because unlike FSR 3 or DLSS 3 they cannot exclude the HUD inside the engine. A modding article on DLSS Enabler describes a per-frame, image-based HUD detector that combines several signals, calls the result "an inference rather than a certainty", and says semi-transparent or animated interfaces can be misjudged. The article returned 403 when fetched, so this is from the search summary. Sources: [Digital Trends on AFMF 2](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/amd-afmf-2-tested/), [Nexus Mods article 238](https://www.nexusmods.com/site/articles/238)

Sky
- Depth Anything's training sets sky disparity to 0, the farthest value, using a segmentation model. Source: [Depth Anything, arXiv 2401.10891](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.10891v1)
- In Depth Anything 3 only the Large-size mono, metric and nested models have a sky segmentation output. Source: [Depth Anything 3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3/main/README.md). Community ONNX exports of DA3-Small drop the sky branch, and exports of the Large models disagree on the sky score threshold, 0.3 in one and 0.5 in another. Search summary. Sources: [zerochocobo DA3 ONNX](https://huggingface.co/zerochocobo/DepthAnything3_ONNX), [Heliosoph DA3 Metric Large ONNX](https://huggingface.co/Heliosoph/da3metric-large-onnx)
- MVS-Synth's ground truth covers sky, which many real datasets miss. Search summary. Source: [MVS-Synth](https://phuang17.github.io/DeepMVS/mvs-synth.html)
- MoGe returns a binary mask of valid pixels with its point map. The README does not say how sky is treated. Source: [MoGe README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/MoGe/main/README.md)

Thin structures and edges
- 17 to 46% of edge points from monocular models are flying points in the Jetson benchmark, against 1 to 9% for stereo. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)
- Depth Pro measures boundary recall on matting datasets of hair and fur, AM-2k and P3M-10k, because that is where models differ most. Source: [Depth Pro](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02073v1)
- XeGTAO's thin occluder compensation is off by default and has mixed results. It helps pipes and chair legs and over-compensates on deep shelves. Source: [XeGTAO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO/master/README.md)

Cuts
- iw3 resets temporal state at detected scene boundaries. Source: [iw3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nagadomi/nunif/master/iw3/README.md)

### Inferences
- A capture-only HUD detector has three signals available. Temporal stability: keep a per-pixel EMA of absolute frame difference and compare it with the frame's overall motion, so a pixel that stays still while the scene moves is HUD. Persistence: require the state to hold for a second or more and decay it when the whole frame is still, such as in a pause menu. Edge character: HUD and text have axis-aligned one-pixel edges with high contrast. Run it at quarter resolution to keep it under 0.1 ms, my estimate. Semi-transparent HUD panels will fail the stability test, as every source above also found, so a user-painted mask in the UIMask style has to stay as the fallback.
- What to do with HUD pixels matters more than finding them. The ReShade convention is to restore the original pixels after the effect chain, which is what `UIMask_Bottom` does. Unishade owns the compositor, so it can do the same with its detected mask. For depth, the HUD region should not carry the model's guess, which is usually a near-plane sticker that casts AO onto the scene. Fill it from the surrounding scene depth with a push-pull or dilation fill at model resolution.
- For sky without a sky head, the cheap signal is disparity under a small fraction of the smoothed near anchor, combined with low local colour variance and connectivity to the top edge of the frame. Then write exactly the far-plane value. Section 3 explains why indoor scenes can produce false sky this way if the model pins its farthest point to zero disparity. Measure before shipping.
- Letterbox bars can be found as full-width rows or full-height columns with near-zero luma and near-zero temporal variance. Crop them before inference so the model's small input is spent on the picture, and write depth 0 into the bars so Glamarye-style menu detection skips them.
- Particles, smoke, glass and water have no single correct depth. A model tends to flip between the surface and what is behind it from frame to frame, so these regions show up as temporal instability. Lowering the temporal filter's trust where per-pixel disparity variance is high, and falling back to a spatially smoothed value there, is the general remedy. I have no source for how any specific model behaves on game particles.
- Fog reduces contrast with distance, which monocular models use as a depth cue, so uniform fog probably helps ordering and hurts edges at range. This is a guess.
- Mirrors and screens inside the scene get either the mirror plane or the reflected scene. For AO the plane is the right answer. Nothing in a post-filter can choose.

### Gaps
- No vendor documentation of HUD detection in Lossless Scaling, AFMF or NVIDIA Smooth Motion was found.
- I could not find how SuperDepth3D or desktop2stereo treat HUD, weapon models or depth post-processing. Searches returned nothing relevant.
- I found no evaluation of monocular depth models on game particles, volumetric fog, glass or mirrors, and nothing on how HUD overlays in the input affect Depth Anything's output for the rest of the frame.
- Which ReShade plugin OmniWorld used to strip UI is not named in the paper. It needs access to the render pipeline, which Unishade does not have.

## 7. Evaluation and datasets

### Takeaway
The established recipe is scale-and-shift alignment in disparity, then boundary F1 from depth-ratio contours, a flying-pixel rate, and a temporal metric. Capturing ground truth from real games through ReShade is a published method. OmniWorld-Game did it for 18.5M frames. The step that is missing from the literature is scoring the shading result itself.

### Cited findings

Metrics
- Depth Pro's boundary metric marks an occluding contour between neighbouring pixels `i, j` when `d(j)/d(i) > 1 + t/100`. Precision and recall compare predicted and ground-truth contours over all neighbouring pairs and are scale-invariant. F1 is computed for `t` from 5 to 25 and averaged with higher thresholds weighted more. Non-maximum suppression within connected components penalises blurry edges. With matting or segmentation masks only recall is computed, and for matting a pixel counts as occluding when alpha exceeds 0.1. Source: [Depth Pro, arXiv 2410.02073](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02073v1)
- Pixel-Perfect Depth's edge metric extracts edge masks from ground-truth depth with a Canny operator and computes Chamfer distance between predicted and ground-truth point clouds near those edges. Source: [arXiv 2601.05246](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.05246)
- The flying-points rate counts predicted edge pixels that fall in front of the nearest or behind the farthest ground-truth surface. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)
- SharpDepth evaluates boundary detail on three synthetic datasets, including Sintel and Spring, with a pseudo depth boundary error. Search summary. Source: [SharpDepth supplemental](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/supplemental/Pham_SharpDepth_Sharpening_Metric_CVPR_2025_supplemental.pdf)
- Affine-invariant disparity is evaluated after a least-squares fit of scale and offset in disparity space. Search summary. Source: [MoGe, arXiv 2410.19115](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.19115v3)
- Reporting AbsRel both raw and with an oracle per-image scale separates scale error from shape error. Source: [arXiv 2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27199v1)
- Normal quality is usually reported as angular RMSE and the fraction of pixels within 11.25 degrees. Source: [AnyUp, arXiv 2510.12764](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12764v2)
- Temporal Alignment Error reprojects the depth of frame `k` into frame `k+1` with the known camera transform and takes AbsRel against the prediction there, averaged in both directions over the sequence. It needs camera poses. Video Depth Anything's training loss compares `|d_{i+1} − d_i|` with `|g_{i+1} − g_i|` where the ground-truth change is under 0.05. VDA-S runs at 9.1 ms per frame on an A100 at 518x518 in FP32, against 60 ms for Depth Anything V2-L. Source: [Video Depth Anything, arXiv 2501.12375](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12375v3)
- OPW, the optical-flow warping error, is the pose-free alternative used by NVDS and STATIC. A 2026 paper shows AbsRel responding erratically as flicker increases and proposes a dedicated temporal metric, TGSE. Search summaries. Sources: [NVDS, arXiv 2307.08695](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.08695), [VDPP, arXiv 2604.06665](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06665)

Datasets with ground-truth depth
- OmniWorld-Game: 96K+ clips, about 18.5M frames, over 214 hours, 1280x720 at 24 FPS. Depth was read during rendering with ReShade while OBS captured synchronised RGB. UI was stripped with a ReShade plugin. The paper names no dataset licence and says game content is used under the games' non-commercial terms. Its ethics statement refers to Rockstar Games content. Source: [OmniWorld, arXiv 2509.12201](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12201v1). The Hugging Face card describes 16-bit depth maps, optical flow, camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and foreground masks. Search summary. Source: [OmniWorld on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/InternRobotics/OmniWorld/blob/4e2191f5169216b672adf07337286385401c285f/README.md)
- MVS-Synth: 120 sequences of 100 frames of urban scenes from Grand Theft Auto V, with complete ground-truth disparity including sky. Research and educational use only. Search summary. Source: [MVS-Synth](https://phuang17.github.io/DeepMVS/mvs-synth.html)
- Sizes and resolutions from OmniWorld's comparison table: MPI Sintel 1024x436, 1K frames. TartanAir 640x480, 1,000K. HyperSim 1024x768, 77K. Dynamic Replica 1280x720, 169K. Spring 1920x1080, 23K. PointOdyssey 960x540, 216K. SeKai-Game 1920x1080, 4,320K frames but no depth. Source: [OmniWorld, arXiv 2509.12201](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12201v1)
- Spring's own paper describes 6000 stereo pairs from 47 sequences at 1920x1080 with ground truth computed at 3840x2160. Virtual KITTI 2 has 21,260 samples at 1242x375. A secondary licence table gives Spring CC BY 4.0, TartanAir CC BY 4.0, Virtual KITTI 2 CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, PointOdyssey CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and Dynamic Replica non-commercial. Sources: [Spring, arXiv 2303.01943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01943v1), [MDE survey, arXiv 2609.01172](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01172)

### Inferences
- A protocol for Unishade, built from the pieces above.
  1. Capture pairs. Use ReShade with depth access in single-player games to save the real depth, and capture the same presented frame as Unishade would see it, HUD on. Record a second pass with the HUD off where the game allows it, so HUD handling can be scored on its own. OmniWorld's method shows this works at scale.
  2. Run Unishade's whole depth pipeline offline on the colour frames, including the upsampler, at the real output resolution.
  3. Align by least squares in disparity. Report the fitted shift over scale, `b/a`, per frame. Its distribution tests the zero-shift assumption in section 3 directly.
  4. Report AbsRel after scale-only alignment and after scale-and-shift alignment. The gap between them is what the mapping costs.
  5. Report Depth Pro's boundary F1 at full resolution, and the flying-points rate. These two respond to the upsampler. Blur lowers F1 through the non-maximum suppression step, and intermediate values raise the flying-points rate.
  6. Report normal error. Reconstruct normals from both depth buffers with the same finite-difference code MXAO uses, after alignment, and report mean and median angle and the fraction under 11.25 degrees, excluding sky and a few pixels either side of ground-truth discontinuities, then separately including them.
  7. Score the task. Run the same AO shader on the real and the predicted depth and compare the AO buffers. This is the number that says whether a change to the pipeline helps what users see.
  8. Temporal. With a static camera, take the per-pixel standard deviation over time of aligned disparity. With motion, use OPW with any optical-flow estimate, since game captures usually lack poses. Track the frame-to-frame change of the fitted scale separately, because it shows up as AO radius and fog pumping.
- Use the same real depth to tune the filter thresholds in section 1 and the percentile and EMA settings in section 3. Each has one or two free parameters and a metric above that responds to it.
- Among public sets, Spring is the closest to Unishade's resolution with pixel-accurate ground truth, though it is one stylised film. MVS-Synth and OmniWorld-Game are real game frames. OmniWorld-Game is 720p and both carry non-commercial or research-only terms, which is fine for evaluation and rules out redistributing derived training data with an MIT project.

### Gaps
- I found no dataset of game frames with ground-truth depth at 1440p or 4K with the HUD present. HUD-on evaluation needs Unishade's own captures.
- I found no published benchmark that scores monocular depth by the quality of AO, DoF or GI computed from it.
- TAE's exact handling of intrinsics and occlusion masks was not in the text I read.
- The search surfaced a GTA V set of 100,000 1920x1080 frames with dense depth, but I could not tell which paper it belongs to, so it is not listed.
- I did not verify RenderDoc-based capture workflows. ReShade capture is the one with a published precedent.

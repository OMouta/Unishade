# Comparable projects that estimate depth from the screen or video in real time

Research date: 2026-11-24. Facts marked "(code)" come from reading the project's source at its default branch on that date. Everything under "Inferences" is my reasoning, not something a source states. Search-result snippets that I could not open in full are marked "(snippet)".

## 1. Real-time desktop and game 2D-to-3D tools driven by monocular depth

### Takeaway
The two open-source references are nagadomi's iw3 (MIT, PyTorch) and lc700x's desktop2stereo (MIT, PyTorch with TensorRT/DirectML/CoreML/OpenVINO/MIGraphX back ends). Both default to a Depth Anything V2 Small class model at roughly 336 to 392 px, both stabilize depth with a plain exponential moving average and no motion compensation, and in both the depth model is not the bottleneck, capture and copies are. Commercial products (XREAL, Viture, Samsung, Acer, Leia) publish almost no engineering detail.

### Cited findings

#### nagadomi/nunif iw3 (offline converter plus `iw3.desktop` realtime mode)

- Licence is MIT for the code. Source: [nunif LICENSE](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/LICENSE), [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- Depth models offered: ZoeDepth (`ZoeD_N/K/NK`), ZoeDepth with Depth Anything metric heads (`ZoeD_Any_N/K`), Depth Anything v1 (`Any_S/B/L`), Depth Anything V2 relative and metric (`Any_V2_S/B/L`, `Any_V2_N_*`, `Any_V2_K_*`), Depth Anything 3 mono (`Any_V3_Mono`, `Any_V3_Mono_01`), Depth Pro (1536 and 1024, distance clipped at 40 m), Distill Any Depth S/B/L, "Ultimate DAV2" S/B/UL, Video Depth Anything S/B/L including metric and streaming variants (`VDA_Stream_*`). Source: [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- The README flags these checkpoints as CC-BY-NC-4.0 and requires manual download: `Any_V2_B/L`, the large metric V2 models, `VDA_B/L` and their metric and streaming variants, Ultimate DAV2. Distill Any Depth is listed as Apache 2.0 but initialized from CC-BY-NC DAV2 weights. Source: [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- The realtime mode defaults to `--depth-model Any_V2_S`, `--divergence 1`, `--convergence 1`, batch size 1, `--stream-fps 15`, `--stream-height 1080`, `--stream-quality 90`. Source: [iw3 desktop.md](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/docs/desktop.md)
- Default depth input for Depth Anything models is a 392 px short side (`prep_lower_bound = resolution or 392`), aspect ratio capped at 4, and the GUI maps 512 to 518 (code). Source: [depth_anything_model.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/depth_models/depth_anything_model.py)
- Runtime is PyTorch with FP16 autocast. There is a `--compile` flag ("compile model if possible"). I found no TensorRT or ONNX path in the README or the argument list (code). Source: [iw3/utils.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/utils.py), [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- Windows capture back ends are `wc_mp` (windows_capture in a separate process), `wc_cuda` (CUDA based, NVIDIA only), `mss`, `pil`. The local viewer tries CUDA to OpenGL interop and falls back to a slower CPU copy. Source: [iw3 desktop.md](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/docs/desktop.md)
- "EMA normalize" smooths only two scalars per frame, the minimum and maximum of the depth map (or only the maximum in "max" mode), then normalizes the whole frame by them. It does not filter per-pixel depth. The class keeps a ring buffer of per-frame min/max over a lookahead window, takes the window's min and max, then applies `v = decay * v + (1 - decay) * new`. Presets named in a code comment: "SimpleMinMaxScaler: decay=0, buffer_size=1", "IncrementalEMAScaler: decay=0.75, buffer_size=1", "WindowEMAScaler: decay=0.9, buffer_size=30" (code). Source: [depth_scaler.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/depth_scaler.py)
- CLI defaults are `--ema-decay 0.75` and `--ema-buffer 30` frames of lookahead. The README recommends decay 0.75 to 0.99 (code). Source: [iw3/utils.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/utils.py), [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- In realtime mode EMA is always on with `buffer_size=1` (no lookahead), under the comment "Use Flicker Reduction to prevent 3D sickness". The GUI labels it "Flicker Reduction", offers decays 0.99/0.95/0.9/0.75/0.5 and preselects 0.9 (code). Source: [desktop/utils.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/desktop/utils.py), [desktop/gui_shared.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/desktop/gui_shared.py)
- `Any_V3_Mono` uses a "max scaler, for VR" and `Any_V3_Mono_01` a "min-max scaler, for anaglyph or 3D TV". Source: [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- Offline, `--scene-detect` runs TransNetV2 shot boundary detection and resets the EMA and Video Depth Anything state at each cut. Source: [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- The author doubts scene cut detection works in games: "in games, large score displays and similar elements may be updated every frame, so it is unclear whether scene change detection would work reliably." Source: [nunif issue 619](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/619)
- Edge dilation ("Edge Fix") works on the disparity map. Each iteration computes an edge weight from the local 3x3 max minus min, standardizes it, clamps to plus or minus 3 sigma, rescales to 0..1, then blends the map with a 3x3 Gaussian blur followed by a 3x3 max-pool dilation: `x = x*(1-w) + dilate(blur(x))*w`. X and Y iteration counts are separate. The CLI default is `[2, 1]`, which is one 3x3 pass plus one horizontal-only pass (code). Source: [dilation.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/dilation.py), [iw3/utils.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/utils.py)
- The README describes the trade-off: 0 disables it and "4 is the most eye-friendly but reduces depth accuracy". GUI tooltip: "Reduce distortion of foreground and background edges". Source: [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md), [desktop/gui_shared.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/desktop/gui_shared.py)
- Mapping functions convert normalized model output to disparity. For relative depth: `none`, `mul_1..3` (a normalized softplus, bias 0.343/0.515/0.687, scale 12, described as "smooth 1.5x/2x/3x"), `inv_mul_1..3`, and `shift_045..shift_30`. The shift mapper turns disparity into distance with an assumed `min_distance` and `max_distance=16`, moves the nearest distance to 1, converts back, and rescales with fixed constants. A code note says not to renormalize with the frame's own min/max there because "This condition is required when using EMA normalization with look-ahead buffers". For metric depth: `div_25..div_1` with `c/(1+c-x)`. `--foreground-scale` from -3 to 3 picks one of seven levels and fractional values blend two mappers. `auto` means `none` for relative models and `div_6` for metric (code). Source: [mapper.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/mapper.py)
- Auto convergence uses a small salient object detection network at 192x192 input, takes the 10th and 90th percentile of depth under the saliency mask and smooths the chosen plane with an EMA (decay 0.9 by default, 0.98 in realtime mode) (code). Source: [convergence_estimator.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/convergence_estimator.py), [desktop/utils.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/desktop/utils.py)
- The Depth Anything 3 path loads a separately trained "DepthAA" network (depth anti-aliasing) and there is a training add-on for it (code). I did not read its architecture. Source: [depth_anything_v3_model.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/depth_models/depth_anything_v3_model.py), [nunif_addon.py](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/nunif_addon.py)
- Stereo synthesis is a learned backward warp (`row_flow_v3` default, `mlbw_l2/l4` layered variants, inpainting variants), with a non-ML `forward_fill` option. Naive backward warp (`grid_sample`) is listed as having "many ghost artifacts". Source: [iw3 README](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/README.md)
- Performance, author's RTX 3070 Ti, March 2025: "the performance of Any_V2_S(resolution=392) itself can achieve about 120 FPS even with batch-size=1" while the desktop pipeline ran at about 14 FPS. Moving off slow JPEG encoding gave 24 FPS and running capture and conversion in parallel gave 48 FPS. Source: [nunif issue 319](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/319)
- An RTX 4090 user reported "over 800fps in the benchmark if I set batch size = 8, but only about 27fps streaming". Source: [nunif issue 319](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/319)
- February 2026, author: "any_v2_s can reach around 160–200 FPS", and skipping depth on alternate frames would improve the whole pipeline by "only about 10-20%" because "depth estimation is performed at a fixed resolution" while the "slowdown caused by higher resolution comes from screen capture, device memory transfer, and image encoding". Source: [nunif issue 619](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/619)
- With a heavier model the picture changes. A user running `VDA_Stream_B` went "from about ~23 to ~42" FPS by reusing the previous depth every other frame, about 70 FPS at a skip of 4, and judged it "doesn't look awful" on a monitor without interpolation. Source: [nunif issue 619](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/619)
- The author's plan for depth at a lower rate than video: compute optical flow between consecutive low-resolution frames with NVIDIA's Optical Flow Accelerator and warp the previous depth forward ("generate DEPTH2 from DEPTH1 using the flow from RGB1 to RGB2"). He notes ML optical flow "can be as heavy as, or even heavier than, small depth estimation model", and that buffering to hide stutter would make "gaming and mouse interaction" difficult. Source: [nunif issue 619](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/619)
- Author's diagnosis of residual flicker on snow, sand and fast motion: "The depth for particles (snow, sand) or motion blur (fast moving objects) is unstable (a depth estimation model issue)" and "The particles are closest objects to the camera, causing the maximum disparity to flicker (a normalization issue)". He says neither is fixable by settings and suggests "using the median disparity instead of the maximum disparity as the reference value", as Elastic3D does. Source: [nunif issue 583](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/583)
- Documented known issue for the realtime mode: text and GUI windows may get poor depth estimates and cause discomfort, the tool targets full-screen images and video. Source: [iw3 desktop.md](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/blob/master/iw3/docs/desktop.md)
- User report on resolution: higher depth resolution "can help with fine/thin details and can clear up smearing along edges", with "a tiny bit more flicker with much higher res depth maps"; Depth Anything 3 at a depth resolution of 1080 with inpainting ran at about 2 fps on an RTX 4080. Native resolutions quoted in the thread: 518 for VDA_L, 504 for DA3 mono. Source: [nunif issue 636](https://github.com/nagadomi/nunif/issues/636)
- A community checkpoint, "Ultimate DAV2 ViT-S", distills DAV2-Large into DAV2-Small using COCO, Unsplash and "high-resolution first-person shooter game screenshots", aimed at 2D-to-3D conversion of games. Two variants: 12 ViT blocks (about 95 MB) and "UL" with 10 blocks (about 81 MB, "Fastest"). Recommended lower bounds 518 px and 392 px. Licence CC-BY-NC-4.0. The card gives no benchmarks and warns that results on "text/UI-heavy game screenshots" may differ. Source: [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/Jokeru1983/Ultimate_DAV2_ViT-S_Gaming)

#### lc700x/desktop2stereo

- MIT, Windows/macOS/Ubuntu, 173 stars at fetch time. Outputs: OpenXR link, local stereo viewer, MJPEG/RTMP streams, 3D monitor mode. Source: [desktop2stereo README](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo)
- Models: Depth Anything V2 S/B/L and metric, Video Depth Anything S/B/L, Depth Anything 3 (small to giant, metric, nested), Depth Anything v1, Distill Any Depth, DPT/MiDaS, ZoeDepth, Depth Pro ("Slow, NOT recommended"). The settings file also lists InfiniDepth Small/SmallPlus/Base/Large. Source: [desktop2stereo README](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo), [settings.yaml](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/settings.yaml)
- Defaults conflict between sources. The README names `Depth-Anything-V2-Small-hf` and depth strength 2.0. The checked-in `settings.yaml` has `Depth Model: Distill-Any-Depth-Small` and `Depth Strength: 4.0`. Both agree on depth resolution 336, anti-aliasing 2, foreground scale 0.5, FP16 on. Source: [desktop2stereo README](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo), [settings.yaml](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/settings.yaml)
- Selectable depth resolutions are multiples of the ViT patch size: 196, 238, 294, 336, 392, 448, 518 for the DINOv2 family and 192 to 512 for InfiniDepth. Source: [settings.yaml](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/settings.yaml)
- Runtimes: PyTorch on CUDA, DirectML (torch-directml), ROCm 6/7, Intel XPU, ZLUDA, MPS. Optional accelerators: torch.compile, TensorRT (via ONNX export), CoreML, OpenVINO, MIGraphX. The README claims these raise output FPS by 30% to 50% and fall back to PyTorch on failure. Source: [desktop2stereo README](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo), [depth.py](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/depth.py)
- Windows capture options: DXGI Desktop Duplication ("Highest FPS, higher CPU temperature"), Windows Graphics Capture, and GPU-resident variants `WindowsCaptureCUDA` (nagadomi's `wc_cuda`, MIT) and `WindowsCaptureROCm`. Source: [desktop2stereo README](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo), [wc_cuda](https://github.com/nagadomi/wc_cuda)
- Post-processing chain, in order (code): per-frame normalization that clips to the 2nd and 98th percentile computed on at most 6,144 subsampled values, then `pow(depth, 1.45)`, then a foreground curve `mid + sign(d-mid) * |d-mid|^(1/(1+scale))` around 0.5, then a separable Gaussian blur with kernel `int(3*strength)|1` and sigma `0.5*strength` (the "Anti-aliasing" setting), then temporal smoothing, then bilinear upsampling to the frame size. Source: [depth.py](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/depth.py)
- Temporal smoothing is a per-pixel EMA with `alpha=0.9`: `prev.lerp_(depth, 1 - alpha)`, so each new depth map contributes 10%. No motion compensation and no scene cut reset, it only resets when the tensor shape changes (code). The normalization range itself is not smoothed over time. Source: [depth.py](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/depth.py)
- Stereo is a plain backward warp with `grid_sample` and reflection padding, no inpainting. The DirectML path replaces it with integer `gather` and carries shims for `nan_to_num`, `quantile` and matrix inverse (code). Source: [depth.py](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/depth.py)
- Benchmarks from release notes, Depth-Anything-V2-Small, defaults, input equal to output resolution. RTX 2070 with WindowsCaptureCUDA in the local viewer: 1920x1080 at 57 FPS and 10 ms, 2560x1440 at 56 FPS and 16 ms, 3840x2160 at 34 FPS and 110 ms. RX 9060 XT with WindowsCaptureROCm: 57 FPS/13 ms, 56 FPS/15 ms, 51 FPS/10 ms. Source: [desktop2stereo v2.4.1 release](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/releases/tag/v2.4.1)
- OpenXR link mode at 3840x2160: RTX 2070 60 FPS at 89 ms, RX 9060 XT 60 FPS at 16 ms. Source: [desktop2stereo v2.4.2 release](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/releases/tag/v2.4.2)
- The older `Benchmark.md` (model not named) compares CUDA and DirectML on one RTX 5070 Ti at 1080p in and out with depth 384: 31 FPS at 0.07 s on CUDA against 28 FPS at 0.12 s on DirectML. At depth 192: 31 FPS/0.06 s against 30 FPS/0.14 s. Apple M1 at 1080p, depth 192: 27 FPS at 0.15 s. Source: [Benchmark.md](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/blob/main/Benchmark.md)
- Bugs users hit: FP16 autocast produced block artifacts with Video Depth Anything metric small (fixed in v2.4.2); the TensorRT export of Video Depth Anything Small came out flat on RTX 50 but not RTX 20 cards (fixed by a model file change); one user's "background is missing" complaint turned out to be the anti-aliasing blur strength. Source: [issue 12](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/issues/12), [issue 16](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/issues/16), [issue 15](https://github.com/lc700x/desktop2stereo/issues/15)

### Inferences
- Every open tool in this class stabilizes the output range, not the geometry. iw3 smooths two scalars. desktop2stereo blends whole depth maps at 10% per frame, which must lag behind moving objects by many frames. Nobody reprojects the previous depth along motion before blending. Unishade's "smoothed peak" is the same idea as iw3's max-mode EMA, so the gap to close is per-pixel, motion-compensated filtering, which neither project has shipped.
- iw3's author names the weakness of peak normalization directly: a few near pixels (particles, a weapon, a HUD element) move the peak and the whole scene pulses. A robust statistic (desktop2stereo's 2/98 percentile, or the median he proposes) with a slow EMA is the cheap fix. Unishade normalizes by a smoothed peak, so it likely has this failure today.
- Stereo warping only needs disparity ordering near edges, so both tools get away with blur and dilation. A ReShade depth buffer feeds shaders that take derivatives of depth, so the blur-heavy recipes here (Gaussian "anti-aliasing", multi-pass dilation) would flatten contact detail that AO needs. Their edge dilation is still worth copying in a guided form: grow the near surface by one or two low-resolution texels before the joint bilateral upsample, so halos land on the background side.
- The measured numbers say the model is cheap and the plumbing is not. Small ViT-S at about 392 px runs at 120 to 200 FPS on a 3070 Ti class card in PyTorch and about 3 ms in TensorRT on a 4090 at 518 px. desktop2stereo's own table puts DirectML at roughly twice the latency of CUDA on the same GPU. For Unishade this points at fixed-shape ONNX exports (the SyLC note about DirectML's CPU fallback is the one concrete DirectML pitfall I found), keeping capture and depth on the GPU, and offering TensorRT or a native-CUDA provider as an option next to DirectML.
- Running depth at a lower rate than the game and warping it forward with optical flow is planned in iw3 but not finished. It only pays off for models heavier than ViT-S.
- FP16 is not safe everywhere. Two separate desktop2stereo bugs came from half precision or the TensorRT export of one specific model on one GPU generation, so a depth sanity probe (variance and finite share, as DLSS5-Feeder does) is worth having.

### Gaps
- I found no public technical description of the depth models or temporal filtering in XREAL, Viture, Samsung, Acer, Leia, Rokid or Apple products. Vendor pages give requirements and frame rates only.
- I found nothing reliable on Virtual Desktop, Bigscreen, 4XVR, DeoVR, Looking Glass tools, Owl3D, or Meta Quest system features doing realtime 2D-to-3D. Searches returned listings without engineering detail.
- I did not find Reddit or forum threads that compare depth models for realtime game conversion with measurements. The user feedback above is from GitHub issues only.
- iw3's handling of HUD/UI is limited to the documented warning. Neither tool masks UI.
- The architecture and cost of iw3's "DepthAA" network were not examined.

## 2. ReShade ecosystem: depth without a depth buffer, and what depth-consuming shaders need

### Takeaway
I found no ReShade add-on or shader that generates depth with a neural network, and no heuristic "fake depth" mode in SuperDepth3D. Unishade's Depth add-on appears to be alone here. The shaders it feeds expect ReShade's linearized depth convention and several rebuild normals and motion from depth, which is where estimated depth will hurt most.

### Cited findings
- Repository searches on GitHub for combinations of "reshade" with "depth anything", "monocular depth", "depth estimation", "ai depth" and "onnx" returned no matching project on 2026-11-24. A web search for ReShade with Depth Anything or MiDaS also returned nothing relevant. Source: [GitHub repository search](https://github.com/search?q=reshade+depth+estimation&type=repositories)
- SuperDepth3D is "Depth Map Based 3D" and reads the game's depth buffer. The string "2D Plus Depth" in the shader is an output format for 2D+Depth displays, not an input mode. Its header says the author's own code is "Copyright (C) Depth3D - All Rights Reserved" and the repository has no licence file, so it is not reusable in an MIT project (code). Source: [Depth3D repository](https://github.com/BlueSkyDefender/Depth3D), [SuperDepth3D.fx](https://github.com/BlueSkyDefender/Depth3D/blob/master/Shaders/SuperDepth3D.fx)
- outmode/rendepth-reshade (MIT, 71 stars) is a 2D-to-3D ReShade plug-in that also uses the game depth buffer; its troubleshooting tells users to check the Z buffer view. Source: [rendepth-reshade](https://github.com/outmode/rendepth-reshade)
- ReShade's depth convention is controlled by global preprocessor definitions: `RESHADE_DEPTH_LINEARIZATION_FAR_PLANE` (default 1000), `RESHADE_DEPTH_INPUT_IS_REVERSED`, `RESHADE_DEPTH_INPUT_IS_LOGARITHMIC`, `RESHADE_DEPTH_INPUT_IS_UPSIDE_DOWN`, plus X/Y scale and offset. Marty's guide calls depth "essential for most 3D effects" and names ambient occlusion, global illumination and depth of field. Source: [Marty's Mods depth guide](https://guides.martysmods.com/reshade/depth/)
- Failure modes the same guide lists for real depth: DLSS/FSR/XeSS can make the buffer "garbled in a checkerboard pattern", dynamic resolution makes "depth effects ... go all over the screen", and some games freeze depth during cutscenes so effects draw over video, for which "There is no way to fix this through ReShade or depth buffer configuration". Source: [Marty's Mods depth guide](https://guides.martysmods.com/reshade/depth/)
- iMMERSE Launchpad is the shared prepass for MXAO, RTGI and ReLight. It produces smoothed normals, "textured normals" with detail sampled from the image, and optical flow that "computes per-pixel motion vectors between consecutive frames using depth, normal, and color data", used to "temporally reproject, accumulate, and denoise" RTGI and MXAO. Source: [Launchpad guide](https://guides.martysmods.com/shaders/immerse/launchpad/)
- Glamarye Fast Effects (MIT) is designed around unreliable depth. Its AO ignores normals: "it doesn't try to adapt to the local normal vector of the pixel", it "picks sample points in a simple circle around the pixel" (2 to 10 samples) and "At least half the samples must be closer for any shade to be cast". Its Fake GI "can be run in 2D mode for when depth info isn't available". Source: [Glamarye README](https://github.com/rj200/Glamarye_Fast_Effects_for_ReShade)
- Glamarye detects menus and video per pixel by depth value: "Skip all processing if depth value is 0", and sky by depth 1. Its benchmark on a GTX 1650 laptop in Witcher 3 at 1080p: baseline 77 FPS, Fast AO only 74, MXAO default 65. Source: [Glamarye README](https://github.com/rj200/Glamarye_Fast_Effects_for_ReShade)
- XeGTAO, a reference AO, takes "depth buffer and (optional) screen space normals" and converts raw depth to view space in a prefilter pass before the AO pass (snippet). Source: [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO)
- ReShade blocks depth access when it detects network activity in online games (forum reports, snippets, not an official statement I opened). Source: [ReShade forum thread](https://www.reshade.me/forum/shader-troubleshooting/4427-depth-based-shaders-wont-work), [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/ReShade)
- Open optical-flow shaders already exist in the ReShade ecosystem and work on color with or without depth: iMMERSE Launchpad, LumeniteFX Kernel (1/8-resolution flow plus a confidence map), VORT (MIT), qUINT/DRME (`texMotionVectors` convention; DRME is CC BY-NC 4.0 and "does not compile on ReShade 6.8"), and DLSS5-Feeder's built-in estimator (MIT). Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)

### Inferences
- Which effects tolerate estimated depth (my reasoning from how the effects use depth, not a measured result):
  - Fog, depth of field and depth-based color grading use depth as a per-pixel scalar. They need correct ordering and stable values, and tolerate low-frequency scale error. The main visible defects will be halos at silhouettes and breathing when normalization moves.
  - AO and screen-space GI use depth differences across a few pixels and, in Launchpad's case, normals. Relative inverse depth from a 300 to 500 px model, upsampled, has almost no true signal at that scale. Flat walls come out with wobbling normals, and anything the joint bilateral filter copies from color edges (texture detail, text) turns into fake geometry. A normal-free AO such as Glamarye's, with a large radius, should survive much better than MXAO or RTGI at small radii.
  - Anything with temporal accumulation (RTGI, MXAO with Launchpad flow) will amplify frame-to-frame depth jitter into flicker or ghosting, because the reprojection assumes geometry is stable between frames.
- Estimated depth is affine-invariant inverse depth. Shaders expect linear depth scaled by the far plane. Unishade needs an explicit disparity-to-linear mapping with a chosen near distance and far cap. iw3's `shift_relative_depth` is a compact worked example of that mapping (assumed minimum distance, maximum ratio of 16) and its note about not renormalizing after the mapping applies to Unishade's smoothed normalization too.
- Glamarye's depth-0 and depth-1 conventions for menus and sky suggest a useful contract. If Unishade can classify HUD pixels (see DLSS5-Feeder's static-pixel detector in section 4) or sky, writing those sentinel values would let existing shaders skip them.
- Cutscenes and UI are unsolved even with real depth, so users of depth effects already expect a hotkey to toggle them. Unishade can do better than real depth here because the model still returns plausible depth for a pre-rendered cutscene.
- A normals output is the obvious next product. Launchpad's "textured normals" show that the ecosystem already invents normal detail from the color image, so an estimated normal map is within what users accept.

### Gaps
- I could not find documentation of the exact precision or derivative assumptions inside MXAO, iMMERSE RTGI or Complete RT. Marty's guides describe configuration, not internals, and the iMMERSE licence terms were not on the pages I read.
- No source measures any ReShade depth effect running on estimated depth. The tolerance ranking above is untested inference.
- The claim that ReShade disables depth in online games comes from forum snippets. I did not open ReShade's own documentation for the exact rule.

## 3. Screen-capture post-processing and frame generation tools without engine data

### Takeaway
None of the capture-level tools I checked uses estimated depth. Lossless Scaling, AFMF and Smooth Motion work from optical flow between finished frames, and their known weak point is the HUD, which they cannot separate from the scene.

### Cited findings
- Lossless Scaling's LSFG "captures two consecutive real frames, analyzes optical flow ... and synthesizes one or more intermediate frames", working "from the final displayed image rather than the game's internal motion vectors". It needs windowed or borderless mode. Its "resolution scale" setting lowers the resolution of motion estimation, not of the output (snippets from a setup guide). Source: [tech-insider setup guide](https://tech-insider.org/lossless-scaling-setup-2026)
- LSFG 3 claimed a 40% GPU load reduction for X2 against LSFG 2 and about 24% lower latency in third-party tests (snippets). Source: [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/pc-gaming/lossless-scaling-3-update-touts-greatly-improved-latency-and-performance-universal-frame-gen-tool-boasts-24-percent-reduced-latency)
- Lossless Scaling moved from DXGI capture to Windows Graphics Capture with a new frame pacing algorithm after Windows 11 24H2 made DXGI capture unreliable (snippet). Source: [SteamDB patch notes](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/16427003)
- AMD AFMF 2 is driver-level and needs no game integration. It disables generation during fast motion ("fallback"), with a "Search Mode" setting. Reviewers note that, unlike FSR 3 or DLSS 3 inside the engine, it cannot exclude the HUD from interpolation, and reported ghosting "in menus and with fine details in high contrast scenes" (snippets). Source: [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/amd-afmf-2-tested/), [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/computing/gpu/amd-just-made-big-improvements-with-afmf-2-a-new-version-of-its-tech-to-make-all-pc-games-run-more-smoothly)
- NVIDIA Smooth Motion is the driver-level equivalent and gained Vulkan support (snippet). On Vulkan it inserts frames inside the driver after any ReShade-level processing, so about half the displayed frames skip post-processing done earlier. Source: [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-adds-vulkan-compatibility-to-smooth-motion-frame-generation-complete-with-a-boost-for-emulators), [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Engine-integrated frame generation needs depth. Intel's XeSS-FG guide lists motion vectors and a depth buffer as core inputs, and AMD's FSR 3 frame interpolation combines game motion vectors with optical flow and reconstructs an estimated depth buffer for the interpolated frame (snippets). Source: [Intel XeSS-FG developer guide](https://intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/xess-fg-developer-guide.html), [AMD frame interpolation docs](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/frame-interpolation/)

### Inferences
- The capture-level tools avoid depth because flow alone is enough for interpolation. Their experience still transfers: a low-resolution flow field, a fallback when motion is too fast, and HUD ghosting as the standing complaint. Unishade can reuse the same flow for depth reprojection.
- Unishade shares Lossless Scaling's capture constraints (borderless or windowed, WGC pacing). Lossless Scaling's move to WGC with custom frame pacing is a sign that DXGI duplication should not be the only capture path on current Windows.
- If a user stacks Smooth Motion or AFMF on the game, Unishade's overlay sees only real frames, so depth work is not doubled. If they stack Lossless Scaling on top of Unishade's overlay, generated frames inherit Unishade's effects. Worth testing, I have no source for either arrangement.

### Gaps
- I found no statement from the Lossless Scaling developer on whether LSFG uses any learned depth internally. No source mentions depth.
- I found nothing usable on Magpie, NVIDIA RTX Video, RTX Dynamic Vibrance, Windows Auto SR or NVIDIA Freestyle's depth filters in this pass. The Freestyle search returned only ReShade forum posts.
- AMD and NVIDIA have not published the internals of AFMF 2 or Smooth Motion in anything I found.

## 4. Feeding estimated depth or motion vectors into upscalers and neural rendering

### Takeaway
The closest project to Unishade's idea on the motion side is DLSS5-Feeder (MIT, created August 2026, about 1,200 stars). It runs real DLSS DLAA and a DLSS 5 neural pass on finished frames using ReShade depth plus optical-flow motion vectors, and its README documents with measurements what works and what does not. Temporal anti-aliasing on finished frames works with estimated vectors. Super resolution does not, because finished frames have no sub-pixel jitter.

### Cited findings
- OptiScaler (GPL-3.0) only replaces upscalers "in games that already support DLSS2+ / FSR2+ / XeSS". It takes the game's own depth and motion vectors. It notes that the Unreal Engine XeSS plug-in "does not provide depth", which breaks swapping to other upscalers. Source: [OptiScaler README](https://github.com/optiscaler/OptiScaler)
- DLSS5-Feeder "builds a complete DLSS DLAA "contract" out of what ReShade already has — the frame being processed, the depth buffer, and estimated optical-flow motion vectors", runs a real DLSS evaluate on a private D3D12 device and copies the result back into the frame. Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- It still needs a "working ReShade depth buffer" from Generic Depth for the game paths. Its motion estimator, though, "uses the depth buffer when there is a stable one and needs none", and its test rig scores it on a 4K scene with no depth buffer. It also targets "a capture, UI over video, menus". Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Its built-in estimator is "Hierarchical block matching on a luma pyramid, down to 2 px cells" at half resolution. Reported error in pixels against LumeniteFX Kernel on the 3D test scene (textured/plain surfaces): moving objects 0.48/0.57 against 0.84/0.96, a pan 0.92/1.54 against 2.52/2.05. Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Estimated vectors beat none by a wide margin. On the 3D scene, "restlessness" (output change along true motion relative to the input, 1.00 is as steady as the input) is 1.38 for DLAA with no vectors and 1.54 with the neural pass, against about 1.04 to 1.08 with LumeniteFX vectors. The README's summary: "The vectors matter more with a neural pass, not less". Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- The gap to exact vectors remains. With oracle vectors "plain DLAA's scene error drops from 0.73 to 0.46", and "A slow pan is where an estimate hurts most: 1.60 times as restless as the input with LumeniteFX, 1.27 with exact vectors", because the estimate is "a few tenths of a pixel out on a slow one — a large share of a motion of 0.6 px". Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Per-pixel refinement rules: a pixel whose neighbourhood did not change, or that "did not move" explains at least twice as well as the provider's vector, gets exactly zero; at borders between motions it tries neighbouring cells and last frame's vector; it snaps to whole-pixel vectors when last frame fetched there equals the pixel. Cost is "on the order of half a millisecond at 4K" on an RTX 5090 (the README calls the figure rough). Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Vector validation reprojects each vector into the previous frame and tests depth (disocclusion), vector consistency and a static hypothesis on "illumination-normalised structure". Failed vectors are zeroed and flagged in a mask. The static test needs two wins in a row (`STATIC_HYSTERESIS`), because deciding afresh each frame made vectors alternate and produced "a flicker that comes and goes". Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Optical flow fails on lighting changes: it "answers a lighting change — a flickering light, a flame — with a confident vector pointing at whatever happened to match". Listed limitation: "A light that flickers faster than DLSS's history converges is averaged into a slow pulse." Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- HUD handling: "the UI is processed with the scene (a UI mask / pre-UI colour capture is future work)". The optional "restore text and HUD" pass marks a pixel as HUD when it "stands still while at least a quarter of the frame and its own surroundings move, and that has the local contrast of lettering or line work", keeps the mark while the pixel is unchanged, and puts the original fine detail back. On the 3D test, HUD error with plain DLAA went from 1.12 to 0.86 (detail only) or 0.43 (whole pixel). Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- 30 fps content shown at 60 Hz makes a temporal model flicker at 30 Hz, since it does not return the same output for a repeated input. The fix detects a repeated frame (up to three in a row) and shows the earlier output again. Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Super resolution failed. "DLSS SR leans on motion vectors far more than DLAA", a Quality/Balanced/Performance mode "cannot be added", and the synthetic-jitter experiment "costs as much as 100% ... and shimmers". Also: "Fractional motion cannot be reprojected losslessly without jitter", so text scrolling at 2.5 px per frame is softer with the right vector than with none. Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- A camera-model fit (track 900 points, fit one camera motion, use it where it explains pixels) helps pans and hurts travelling shots, and is off by default. "While the camera travels there is no single motion to fit without depth". Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Operational details worth copying: a probe reads back the motion and depth actually sent every 600 frames from an older completed copy, logs non-zero share, min/max/mean/variance and finite share, and warns when "sampled depth is flat", without a per-frame GPU stall. A test rig (`tools/fx-testbed`) renders synthetic 4K scenes with known vectors through the real chain. Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- The README states MIT for `src/`, `shaders/`, `host/`. GitHub's licence detector reports "NOASSERTION". It depends on closed, community-distributed components (the `renodx-dlss5` add-on from the RenoDX Discord, NVIDIA's `nvngx_dlssnr.dll`) and says the neural model runs on RTX 50 only. Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- ShortFuse's `renodx-dlss` add-on, by the same README's account, hooks the DLSS calls a game already makes and "sits at Auto: Waiting" in games without DLSS. It is distributed on Discord, not GitHub. Source: [DLSS5-Feeder README](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- A small related experiment, taowen/dlss5-as-inpainting (3 stars, no licence), describes "Depth3D stereo reprojection using DLSS 5 as a temporal right-eye inpainting enhancer". Source: [dlss5-as-inpainting](https://github.com/taowen/dlss5-as-inpainting)

### Inferences
- DLSS5-Feeder proves half of what Unishade would need for "DLSS without the engine": estimated motion vectors are good enough for DLAA-style temporal accumulation, with measurable loss on slow pans. It always had real depth for game paths. Nobody has yet shown DLSS or FSR running on estimated depth plus estimated motion from a captured window, and Unishade is in a position to try, since it has both an estimated depth buffer and access to the same ReShade flow shaders.
- The hard limit is jitter, not depth. A captured finished frame carries no sub-pixel samples, so upscaling by a temporal upscaler cannot gain detail. Unishade should treat any such feature as anti-aliasing and stabilization, not as super resolution.
- The validation recipe (reproject, test depth and luma, require hysteresis, keep a trust mask) is directly reusable for temporal filtering of Unishade's estimated depth. The depth test there used real depth. With estimated depth the luma and static tests have to carry more weight.
- The static-pixel HUD detector is the best concrete idea I found for HUD handling without engine data. It needs motion elsewhere in the frame to work, so it fails on a still camera at startup. Combining it with persistence across frames, as the README describes, covers most gameplay.
- Their synthetic test bed with oracle vectors is the right way to tune a depth pipeline too: render a known scene, feed the color through the real chain, and score estimated depth and its temporal stability against ground truth.

### Gaps
- I could not read the RenoDX DLSS add-ons directly because they are distributed over Discord. Everything about them comes from the DLSS5-Feeder README.
- "DLSS 5" and its neural rendering add-on postdate much of my background knowledge. I report the README's claims and did not verify them against NVIDIA documentation.
- I found no attempt to run FSR, XeSS or DLSS on captured video with estimated depth, and nothing on PureDark-style mods using estimated inputs. NVIDIA RTX Video Super Resolution was not covered by any source I retrieved.

## 5. Web, mobile and AR real-time depth deployments with public engineering write-ups

### Takeaway
The most relevant published technique is Google's ARCore depth-from-motion pipeline, which stabilizes depth by tracking a bilateral grid over time instead of a depth map. I could not retrieve usable write-ups for Snap, Niantic, TikTok or Apple's Cinematic mode in this pass.

### Cited findings
- The ARCore Depth API "uses our depth-from-motion algorithms to generate a depth map with a single RGB camera" and launched in ARCore 1.18 on "hundreds of millions of compatible Android devices". Time-of-flight sensors "are not required" but improve quality. Occlusion is the headline use. Source: [Google Developers blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a-new-wave-of-ar-realism-with-the-arcore-depth-api/)
- The underlying paper is Valentin et al., "Depth from Motion for Smartphone AR" (ACM TOG 2018), which combines PatchMatch Stereo and HashMatch ideas and contributes "New extensions of the bilateral solver [Barron and Poole 2016] for depth post-processing" (snippet). Source: [SIGGRAPH archive entry](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=198038)
- The associated patent describes the temporal filter: "Instead of tracking a depth map, a single bilateral grid of estimated depths is tracked, and this bilateral grid is repeatedly updated using the output of a single-image bilateral solver instance on each incoming frame", which makes the smoothing "invariant to small camera or scene motion, without the need to explicitly estimate per-pixel motion between frames" (snippet). Source: [US11145075](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11145075)
- ARCore 1.24 added a Raw Depth API that exposes unsmoothed depth "along with per-pixel confidence images" (snippet). Source: [Google Developers blog, May 2021](https://developers.googleblog.com/2021/05/unlock-use-cases-and-increase-developer-velocity-with-new-capabilities-in-arcore.html)
- Recent edge-deployment research states the trade-off the same way Unishade faces it: per-frame models give "temporal inconsistencies", temporal models are "computationally demanding", and foundation models "rely on heavy backbones and high-resolution inference". AsyncMDE pairs a heavy model that runs occasionally with a light model at high frame rate (snippets). Source: [AsyncMDE, arXiv 2603.10438](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.10438v1)
- A 2026 paper adds a small causal recurrent normalization module to a frozen backbone and reports up to 14% better temporal stability while keeping single-frame accuracy (snippet; the search summary did not make clear which of two listed papers this is). Source: [arXiv 2604.01791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01791)

### Inferences
- The ARCore trick maps well onto Unishade's existing joint bilateral upsample. Keep a low-resolution bilateral grid (x, y, luma) of depth, blend each new frame's splatted depth into it with an EMA, and slice it with the current frame's color. Depth then follows color edges as they move a few pixels, without optical flow. This is the cheapest motion-tolerant temporal filter I found described. Large motions still need flow or a reset.
- Exposing a confidence channel next to depth, as ARCore's raw API does, would let consuming shaders fade AO and GI where the estimate is unstable (recent disocclusion, failed temporal validation, HUD).
- The "heavy model occasionally, light model every frame" pattern and the "learned normalization with memory" pattern both attack the same two problems the iw3 author named: model instability and normalization flicker.

### Gaps
- The DepthLab paper (UIST 2020) PDF could not be parsed in this environment, so I have no cited numbers for ARCore's depth resolution, frame rate or per-feature cost.
- No retrievable engineering write-ups for Snapchat or TikTok depth lenses, MediaPipe depth, Niantic Lightship depth, or Apple's Portrait/Cinematic mode in this pass. These remain open.
- I did not read the AsyncMDE or recurrent-normalization papers in full. The statements above are from search summaries.

## 6. Learned depth or G-buffer estimation on rendered frames

### Takeaway
Learned G-buffer estimation from video exists (DiffusionRenderer, RGBX-Next, RenderFlow's adapter), but all of it is diffusion or flow-matching based and none of the sources I read gives a frame rate close to what a game overlay needs. A cheap learned depth-plus-normals model is more likely to come from the monocular depth family than from inverse-rendering models.

### Cited findings
- DiffusionRenderer (NVIDIA, 2025) uses video diffusion models for inverse rendering, "mapping input RGB videos to intrinsic properties", and for forward rendering from G-buffers. It is multi-step (snippet). Source: [arXiv 2501.18590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.18590)
- RenderFlow (Zhang, Liu, Schroers, Zhang; CVPR 2026) is "end-to-end, deterministic, single-step" flow matching for rendering from G-buffers, claims "near real-time performance", and adds an adapter module for intrinsic decomposition as an inverse task. The abstract page gives no timings. Source: [arXiv 2601.06928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06928)
- RGBX-Next (Zeng, Salvi, Wu, Novák, Lin, Hadadan, Sheng, Pottorff, Liu, Ramamoorthi, Yan, Hašan; arXiv 2608.13929) is "a unified generative framework for forward and inverse rendering" that estimates G-buffers from "images, videos, and streams" by finetuning diffusion transformers. The abstract gives no speed figures and the authors say they will release models. Source: [arXiv 2608.13929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13929)
- Game-domain depth finetuning has been done by a hobbyist: the "Ultimate DAV2" student was distilled on a mix that includes first-person shooter screenshots (see section 1). Source: [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/Jokeru1983/Ultimate_DAV2_ViT-S_Gaming)
- Existing ONNX and engine ports that could seed a native pipeline: fabio-sim/Depth-Anything-ONNX (Apache-2.0, 449 stars), ZInkzach/Video-Depth-Anything-ONNX (Apache-2.0, C++ ONNX Runtime engine for Video Depth Anything), Akiya-Research-Institute/Depth-Anything-UE (MIT, Depth Anything ONNX inside Unreal Engine). Source: [Depth-Anything-ONNX](https://github.com/fabio-sim/Depth-Anything-ONNX), [Video-Depth-Anything-ONNX](https://github.com/ZInkzach/Video-Depth-Anything-ONNX), [Depth-Anything-UE](https://github.com/Akiya-Research-Institute/Depth-Anything-UE)
- FlashDepth (ICCV 2025) is titled "Real-time Streaming Video Depth Estimation at 2K Resolution" (title only, from search results). Source: [arXiv 2504.07093](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07093v1)

### Inferences
- Games are the easiest domain to get ground truth for. A renderer or a ReShade capture with Generic Depth yields perfectly aligned color, depth and (via Launchpad or engine) normals, including HUD overlays. A ViT-S student distilled or finetuned on such captures, with a normals head, looks feasible for a small team and would target the weaknesses the Ultimate DAV2 card admits (text and UI). I found no project that has trained on game captures with real depth buffers as labels.
- Diffusion-based G-buffer estimators are not candidates for a per-frame overlay in 2026 on the evidence here. They could serve as offline teachers for a small student.
- Licence matters for anything Unishade ships. DAV2 Small and DA3 Small/Base are Apache-2.0 per the sources above, while the larger DAV2 and Video Depth Anything checkpoints and the gaming-tuned student are CC-BY-NC.

### Gaps
- No timings for RenderFlow's decomposition adapter or RGBX-Next's stream mode were available from the abstract pages. The full papers need reading before ruling them in or out.
- I did not survey depth-plus-normal monocular models (Metric3D v2, MoGe-2, Lotus, StableNormal, DSINE) in this pass. They belong to the model survey, not this project survey.
- I did not read FlashDepth beyond its title, so its speed and hardware claims are uncited here.

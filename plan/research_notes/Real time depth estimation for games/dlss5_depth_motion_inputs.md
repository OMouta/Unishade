# DLSS 5 (DLSSNR) depth and motion vector inputs, and how temporal upscalers behave with estimated inputs

State as of 24 November 2026. Three evidence classes appear below and each finding says which one it is.

- NVIDIA documented. NVIDIA web pages, the public NVIDIA/DLSS and NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline repositories, and NVIDIA's own open-source RTX Remix runtime (dxvk-remix), which integrates DLSS 5 through NGX.
- Reverse engineered. Community projects that read strings, weights and behaviour out of `nvngx_dlssnr.dll`. NVIDIA has not confirmed any of it.
- Measured by a third party. Community A/B reports and press benchmarks.

Sources marked "search summary" were seen only as a search result digest. I did not read the page itself.

## What is DLSS 5, and what do NVIDIA's public materials and SDK document about it?

### Takeaway
DLSS 5 is a same-resolution neural relighting pass ("3D-Guided Neural Rendering") that runs after upscaling on an SDR, display-referred frame. NVIDIA's public text names only color and motion vectors as inputs, there is no public programming guide or header for it, and the best primary description of the real interface is NVIDIA's own RTX Remix source, which passes color, motion vectors, depth and a control mask, with no jitter and no camera matrices.

### Cited Findings

Announcement, availability, hardware (NVIDIA documented unless noted)
- NVIDIA announced DLSS 5 at GTC on 16 March 2026 and called it its biggest graphics step since real-time ray tracing in 2018 (search summary). [NVIDIA Newsroom](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-dlss-5-delivers-ai-powered-breakthrough-in-visual-fidelity-for-games)
- It shipped on 1 September 2026 in NBA 2K27, "available starting now", for all GeForce RTX 50 Series desktop and laptop GPUs and for GeForce NOW Ultimate on RTX 5080 rigs. It needs Game Ready Driver 616.64 WHQL. [NVIDIA GeForce, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)
- The feature is "an optional and independent feature" that runs as the final rendering stage and works with rasterization, ray tracing and path tracing. The model runs on one GPU at up to 4K on Tensor Cores. [NVIDIA GeForce, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)
- NVIDIA reports a "5X performance gain in just six months" between the dual RTX 5090 demo and the single-card release. [NVIDIA GeForce, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)
- The October update halves GPU overhead again ("now 10X faster overall compared to when it was announced in March"), cuts the model to roughly 118 million parameters, a 24% smaller footprint, and reduces 4K VRAM use by 20%. NVIDIA gives no absolute milliseconds or megabytes. [NVIDIA GeForce, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-faster-ai-model-hogwarts-legacy-rtx-spark-laptops/)
- Rollout after NBA 2K27 was RTX Spark laptops and the new model on 16 October, RTX Remix on 23 October, Hogwarts Legacy on 5 November, and Where Winds Meet "in late November". [NVIDIA GeForce, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-faster-ai-model-hogwarts-legacy-rtx-spark-laptops/)
- Integration paths NVIDIA names are Streamline or an Unreal Engine 5 plugin. [NVIDIA GeForce, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)

What it does to the image and what it takes in (NVIDIA documented)
- "DLSS 5 takes a frame's color and motion vectors as input." It is one frame in, one frame out, deterministic, and uses engine motion vectors for temporal stability. Depth is not mentioned anywhere in the article. [NVIDIA GeForce, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)
- The model is described as trained to recognize "engine data such as color, surface albedo, detailed lighting, and surface normals". That describes training, not runtime inputs. [NVIDIA GeForce, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)
- "Using frame color and motion vectors, the model is designed to add lifelike lighting and material detail", and "richer source data, like ray-traced or path-traced lighting, yields dramatically more-accurate results". [NVIDIA Developer blog, 22 Sept 2026](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/whats-new-for-game-developers-dlss-5-with-3d-guided-neural-rendering-nvidia-ace-updates-and-new-rtx-kit-capabilities/)
- Developer controls NVIDIA names are Structure Intensity for high-frequency detail such as "ambient occlusion, reflections, and subsurface scattering", Tone Intensity for low-frequency lighting and color where zero shows the frame's exact colors, a choice among several models that can be mixed per scene, semantic AI masking, and engine-level masking. Visual Concepts used "a per pixel uplift control mask to fine tune detail on characters". [NVIDIA GeForce, 1 Sept 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/)

Public SDK state (NVIDIA documented, checked on GitHub on 24 Nov 2026)
- The public DLSS SDK header has only an anonymous slot, `NVSDK_NGX_Feature_Reserved18 = 18`. The `include/` directory has headers for DLSS SR, RR (`dlssd`) and FG (`dlssg`) and none for `dlssnr`. [NVIDIA/DLSS nvsdk_ngx_defs.h](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/include/nvsdk_ngx_defs.h)
- The SDK `doc/` folder has the DLSS Programming Guide, the DLSS-RR Integration Guide and the DLSS-FG Programming Guide. There is no DLSS 5 guide. The 310.9.1 and 310.9.16 release notes do not mention DLSS 5. [NVIDIA/DLSS releases](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/releases)
- Public Streamline does define `kFeatureDLSS_NR = 1004` and three buffer tags. `kBufferTypeUpliftInputColor = 70` is "Input color for neural-net 'uplift' passes". `kBufferTypeUpliftOutputColor = 71` "May alias kBufferTypeUpliftInputColor (in-place uplift)". `kBufferTypeUpliftControlMask = 72` is "Optional - 4-channel control mask consumed by uplift passes". There is no `sl_dlss_nr.h` and no programming guide for it in `docs/`. [Streamline sl_core_types.h](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/include/sl_core_types.h)

NVIDIA's own integration in RTX Remix (NVIDIA authored source, the closest thing to documentation)
- dxvk-remix includes `nvsdk_ngx_defs_dlssnr.h` and `nvsdk_ngx_helpers_dlssnr_vk.h`, queries `NVSDK_NGX_Parameter_DLSSNR_Available`, `_NeedsUpdatedDriver`, `_MinDriverVersionMajor/Minor` and `_FeatureInitResult`, and creates the feature with `NVSDK_NGX_DLSSNR_Create_Params` holding only `Width` and `Height` set to the display size. [dxvk-remix rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp)
- The evaluation struct `NVSDK_NGX_VK_DLSSNR_Eval_Params` receives `pInColor`, `pInOutput`, `pInMVec`, `pInDepth`, `pInControlMask`, `InReset`, `InDepthInverted = 0`, `InEnabled = 1`, `InIntensity`, `InLocalToneStrength`, `InLocalStructureStrength`, `InMaskBlendMode`, `InStyle`, `InUseAutoMask`, `InSkinStructureStrength`, `InMVecScaleX/Y`, and separate subrect base and size for color, output, motion vectors, depth and control mask. Remix reads the camera jitter into its settings struct but never passes it to NGX. No matrices, near or far planes, or exposure are passed. [dxvk-remix rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp)
- Remix comments the motion input as "Screen-space motion vectors are expressed in render-pixel units" and sets the scale to 1, 1. Motion vectors and depth come from the render-resolution G-buffer, or from the Ray Reconstruction guide copies when RR is the upscaler, while color and output are display size. [dxvk-remix rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.cpp)
- Remix runs the pass after DLSS SR or RR and before bloom, motion blur and the final tone mapper. It first runs a fast tone map and afterwards an inverse tone map, with the comment "DLSS-NR operates in SDR space; inverse tone mapping restores HDR for downstream post-processing." [dxvk-remix rtx_context.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_context.cpp)
- Remix maps its UI to the parameters this way. "Structure Intensity" drives `InLocalStructureStrength` and "Tone Intensity" drives `InLocalToneStrength`, both sliders 0 to 1. "Model A/B/C" drives `InStyle`. "Enable Auto Mask" drives `InUseAutoMask` and switches `InMaskBlendMode` between `Mask_Blend_Mode_Min` and `_None`. "Character Intensity" drives `InSkinStructureStrength` and Remix forces it to 0 when the auto mask is off. [dxvk-remix rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.cpp)
- Remix sets `NVSDK_NGX_Parameter_DLSSNR_Hint_Render_Preset` to `Preset_B` with the comment "Preset B selects the lightweight network independently of the appearance model", and can query the SDK's VRAM allocation through a stats callback. [dxvk-remix rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp)
- The Remix control mask is an `R8G8B8A8_UNORM` texture at render resolution. [dxvk-remix rtx_resources.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_resources.cpp)

Reverse-engineered parameter list and model facts
- Keys read out of the DLL strings match what Unishade sets, plus more. `DLSSNR.Color`, `.Output`, `.Depth`, `.MVec`, `.DepthInverted`, `.MVecScaleX/Y`, `.Reset`, `.Enabled`, `.Width`, `.Height`, `.Hint.Render.Preset`, `.Style`, `.Intensity`, `.LocalToneStrength`, `.LocalStructureStrength`, `.SkinStructureStrength`, `.UseAutoMask`, `.UICorrection`, and subrect base and size keys for color, output, depth and motion. The author notes "The public NGX SDK does not describe this feature" and that "Everything below was recovered from the shipping DLL". [video2dlssnr nr.h](https://github.com/DaniilSokolyuk/video2dlssnr/blob/HEAD/src/nr.h)
- A hook that logs the resources a real integration passes lists eight resource keys as "verified in the original DLSSNR binary": `DLSSNR.Color`, `.Output`, `.Depth`, `.MVec`, `.ControlMask`, `.UI`, `.UIAlpha`, `.Backbuffer`. [dlss5-on-amd-9070xt-porting](https://github.com/lmxxf/dlss5-on-amd-9070xt-porting/blob/HEAD/Development/native_ngx_input_contract.cpp)
- A reconstructed header states that "NGX silently ignores parameters it does not recognise", that `DLSSNR.UI`, `.UIAlpha` and `.UICorrection` "let it separate a composited HUD from the scene", that `DLSSNR.ControlMask` "masks the effect per pixel", that 310.8.0 rejects every GPU architecture below 0x1B0 (Blackwell), and that `DLSSNR.GlobalToneStrength` exists only in `sl.dlss_nr.dll`. [OpenRemix nvsdk_ngx_defs_dlssnr.h](https://github.com/OpenRenderCollective/OpenRemix/blob/HEAD/src/native/upscale/nvsdk_ngx_defs_dlssnr.h)
- Style values are 0 Default, 1 Natural "gentler, keeps skin tone", 2 Cinematic "less shine". Intensity, local structure and local tone are used over 0 to 2 with default 1. Skin structure uses -1 for the model default. [video2dlssnr README](https://github.com/DaniilSokolyuk/video2dlssnr)
- A PyTorch reimplementation reproduces NVIDIA's output at 45 to 49 dB PSNR and describes the 310.8 network as a U-Net of Swin and vision-transformer blocks, 71 blocks, 152 layers, 145.8 M FP8 parameters. The first block lifts the 3-channel frame to 32 features "with seeded noise and the conditioning". The last block maps features to 3 channels "blended with the frame by the intensity". [dlssnr-pytorch](https://github.com/mauri870/dlssnr-pytorch)
- The same project says of its own port that `depth`, `motion`, `history` and `reset` "are accepted ... and are ignored" because it computes single frames, and that "NVIDIA's library blends in the previous frame's output through history features and motion vectors". [dlssnr-pytorch](https://github.com/mauri870/dlssnr-pytorch)
- From 310.9.16.0 the DLL holds two networks. `DLSSNR.Hint.Render.Preset` 1 selects the original (`WEIGHTS_HT`) and 2 selects the lighter one (`WEIGHTS_CC_SMALL_FP8`, 113.3 M parameters, 31 blocks), which is also the default with no preset. The lighter network's edit is weaker, its output is closer to the input (28.9 dB against 26.4 dB at 1080p). [dlssnr-pytorch](https://github.com/mauri870/dlssnr-pytorch)
- An independent measurement agrees. On 310.9.16 preset 1 is byte-identical to 310.8, and presets 0, 2 and 3 are identical to each other and give the lighter network. On 310.8 every preset value renders the same. [dlss5-video-player runtime report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/runtime-310916-20261031/REPORT.md)
- RenoDX's add-on calls `DLSSNR.UseAutoMask` the "Character mask" and says it "also changes the rest of the picture slightly: brightness by about 2 percent". [dlss5-video-player control mask report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/control-mask-20261016/REPORT.md)

Cost and memory (measured by third parties)
- On an RTX 5090 with 310.9.16, the model costs 3.78 ms at 1080p and 9.63 ms at 4K for the original network, and 2.42 ms and 6.04 ms for the lighter one. Peak process VRAM was about 1.1 GiB at 1080p and 2.6 GiB at 4K. [dlss5-video-player runtime report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/runtime-310916-20261031/REPORT.md)
- Whole-pipeline cost for a live video session on an RTX 5090 was about 6.8 ms at 1080p, 9.9 ms at 1440p and 23 ms at 4K. An RTX 4080 SUPER on a modified runtime measured 15.3 ms at 1080p. [dlss5-video-player README](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player)
- TechSpot estimated the launch model at about 7.9 ms on an RTX 5090, 22.1 ms on an RTX 5070 and 37.8 ms on an RTX 5060 at 4K, and the October model at slightly more than twice as fast (search summary). [TechSpot launch test](https://www.techspot.com/article/3170-real-dlss-5-performance/), [TechSpot follow-up](https://www.techspot.com/article/3190-dlss-5-performance-v2/)
- Digital Foundry measured NBA 2K27 at 4K dropping from 128.4 to 62.7 fps on an RTX 5090 with the launch model (search summary). [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/First-DLSS-5-game-tested-DLSS-5-humbles-RTX-5090-with-over-50-performance-loss.1391978.0.html)
- The DLL first appeared publicly as a 158 MB `nvngx_dlssnr.dll`, version 310.8.0.0, inside an early-access NBA 2K27 build (search summary). That is the version Unishade calls. [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/113305/nvidias-dlss-5-neural-rendering-tech-has-been-found-in-the-first-publicly-accessible-game/index.html)

### Inferences
- The NGX names map to NVIDIA's marketing names as follows. `LocalStructureStrength` is "Structure Intensity", `LocalToneStrength` is "Tone Intensity", `SkinStructureStrength` is "Character Intensity", `UseAutoMask` is the "semantic AI masking" of characters, `ControlMask` is the "per pixel uplift control mask", and `Style` is the choice between look models. The Remix UI labels support every pairing except the last two, which rest on naming alone.
- `Style` is probably a conditioning input to one network, not a set of separate weights. The reimplementation takes `style` as a control of a single weight file, while the preset key is what swaps weights. NVIDIA's phrase "several AI models, with different parameter weights" may describe the preset, the style, or both.
- The network proper sees only the color frame plus scalar conditioning. Depth and motion vectors feed the temporal path that warps and blends the previous output. This follows from the 3-channel pre-block and the reimplementation's description, and nobody has published the temporal path, so treat it as a working model.
- Unishade's input matches what the model expects in one respect. Captured SDR, tone-mapped frames are the color space Remix deliberately converts to before calling the feature. The model does not want jitter, exposure or camera matrices, so their absence in a capture pipeline costs nothing on this feature.
- Since NGX ignores unknown keys silently, a misspelled or unsupported `DLSSNR.*` key in Unishade will fail without any error. Any new key needs an output-difference test.
- Unishade could bind `DLSSNR.ControlMask` to protect HUD regions, but the meaning of its four channels is not documented anywhere I found.

### Gaps
- No NVIDIA document states the format, units or convention of `DLSSNR.Depth` or `DLSSNR.MVec`. Everything about them below comes from NVIDIA's Remix code or from reverse engineering.
- The official `nvsdk_ngx_defs_dlssnr.h` is included by dxvk-remix but is not in the public NVIDIA/DLSS repository. I did not find where NVIDIA distributes it.
- The meaning of the control mask channels, of `MaskBlendMode`, and of `UICorrection` with `DLSSNR.UI` and `UIAlpha` is undocumented.
- NVIDIA publishes no absolute frame time or VRAM figures. Minimum driver claims conflict. NVIDIA says 616.64, one community tool says the DLLs declare 615.00, another uses 610.47 as its floor, and a reconstructed header says 570.
- I could not confirm NVIDIA's reported statement that official RTX 40 support is coming. Search results conflict and none link a primary source.

## How do modders run DLSS 5 outside supported games, and what do they report with missing or estimated depth and motion?

### Takeaway
Running DLSS 5 on captured windows, desktops and video with estimated motion and fake depth is now common, and at least eight open projects do exactly what Unishade plans. They agree that it works, that motion vectors matter far more than depth, that zero motion is acceptable for static content but ghosts under camera motion, and that the HUD gets processed with the scene.

### Cited Findings

Routes in games
- For games that already ship DLSS, the RenoDX DLSS 5 add-on intercepts the game's DLSS Super Resolution evaluation, takes "its colour, depth and motion resources", then creates and evaluates feature 18 itself on its own parameter block. [dlss5-video-player control mask report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/control-mask-20261016/REPORT.md)
- There are two different RenoDX add-ons. Krish's `renodx-dlss5.addon64` is the neural consumer most tools use and is closed source and community distributed. ShortFuse's `renodx-dlss.addon64` builds the DLSS request itself and conflicts with the feeder route. [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- For games without DLSS, DLSS5-Feeder builds a synthetic DLAA request on a private D3D12 device. It sends the finished backbuffer with "render size = output size, no jitter", a depth texture `DLSS5_Depth` in R32F that "copies the raw hardware depth with ReShade's orientation fixes", and motion `DLSS5_MV` in RG16F in pixels, converted from the provider's delta-UV where `prev_uv = uv + mv`. [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- The Feeder's recommended motion source is its own estimator, "Hierarchical block matching on a luma pyramid, down to 2 px cells, for finished pictures of any kind", at half resolution. Its refine pipeline costs about half a millisecond at 4K on an RTX 5090. [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- An Igor's Lab guide says the Feeder result "depends heavily on whether ReShade can access correct depth information" (search summary). [Igor's Lab](https://www.igorslab.de/en/install-dlss-5-reshade-compatible-games/)
- An OptiScaler fork reports a fix for games where NR said "The game's depth or movement data is not supported", and added depth alignment settings and a manual depth size (search summary). [OptiScaler-F5-DLSSNR-Multipass releases](https://github.com/janblade/OptiScaler-F5-DLSSNR-Multipass/releases)
- The official 310.8 DLL refuses RTX 20, 30 and 40 cards with `feature 18 create failed with 0xbad00001`. Community builds patch that check, and the patched DLL loses NVIDIA's signature. [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder), [dlss5-video-player THIRD_PARTY](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/THIRD_PARTY.md)

What they report when motion is estimated or wrong
- Feeder limitations read "Estimated motion vectors → temporal artifacts in fast motion; the UI is processed with the scene", and its beta notes expect "some ghosting in fast motion, softness on thin moving geometry". [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- Optical flow "answers a lighting change with a wrong-but-confident vector" around flames and flicker, and "A light that flickers faster than DLSS's history converges is averaged into a slow pulse." The Feeder runs a validation step with four tests (static hypothesis, luma, depth, consistency) that zeroes failing vectors and masks them. [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- The Feeder warns that setting its depth tolerance to 0 "rejects effectively every vector, which is stable but motionless". [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- In a Stray add-on that feeds engine buffers, handing the model zero motion for the static world while the camera moves produced "ghosting and over-smoothing that accumulates over seconds". The author rates exact-zero vectors on invalid texels as worse than passing the raw, wrongly encoded velocity buffer. [stray-dlssnr](https://github.com/lunks/stray-dlssnr)

DLSS 5 on flat video and captured frames
- A Vulkan layer runs feature 18 on presented frames with `DLSSNR.Depth` left as `nullptr`, and motion from `VK_NV_optical_flow` written as `R16G16_SFLOAT` "in source-pixel units, current-frame-to-previous-frame", with `MVecScaleX/Y = 1`. "If optical flow is unavailable, the helper falls back to zero motion vectors and continues without disabling neural processing." [DLSS5VKLayer](https://github.com/bmitch87/DLSS5VKLayer)
- A ComfyUI bridge runs still images with `DLSSNR.Reset = 1` and `DLSSNR.MVec = null`. For video, frame 0 gets `Reset = 1` and an explicit zero field, and later frames get NVIDIA Optical Flow output as normalized UV in `R16G16_FLOAT` with `DLSSNR.MVecScale = (width, height)`. "No sign flip is applied", since flow from current to previous is already the reprojection direction. [ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR architecture](https://github.com/lisitskyaa/ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR/blob/HEAD/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- That bridge estimates motion from the raw input frames, not the NR output, to avoid "a feedback loop where model-generated detail could alter the next frame's motion estimate". [ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR architecture](https://github.com/lisitskyaa/ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR/blob/HEAD/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- A DaVinci Resolve plugin offers flow method None, AMDOF or NVOF, and "None supplies zero motion while NR continues". Its external motion input wants current-to-previous vectors in source pixels, positive X right and positive Y down. It adds its own anti-flicker stage with five modes. [DaVinci-Resolve-DLSS5](https://github.com/SAOG0721/DaVinci-Resolve-DLSS5)
- An OBS filter by the same author recommends starting with one pass, full inference size, and optical flow and anti-flicker both off. [OBS-DLSS5-Neural-Filter](https://github.com/SAOG0721/OBS-DLSS5-Neural-Filter)
- A Magpie fork, which captures a window and redraws it like Unishade, ships DLSSNR as an effect and states the limit plainly. Magpie "processes complete window images without access to the game engine's full native motion vectors, depth, exposure or separated UI", and "Image processing can affect text and UI along with the scene; temporal effects may also produce ghosting or other artifacts." [magpie-dlss5-amd README](https://github.com/A-ENTROPY/magpie-dlss5-amd)
- NeuralScreen applies the pass to the whole desktop. It reports 25 to 30 ms pipeline latency, caps processing at 2560×1440, and on an RTX 5070 Ti at 4K goes from 45.7 to 72.6 fps by running the network at reduced resolution and compositing the result onto the original frame. [NeuralScreen](https://github.com/perseval-BLR/NeuralScreen)
- video2dlssnr notes that "The model was trained on SDR game frames and knows nothing about PQ or HLG", so it shows the model an SDR proxy and applies the edit back as a residual. It resets history on scene cuts. [video2dlssnr](https://github.com/DaniilSokolyuk/video2dlssnr)
- The DLSS 5 Video Player says of its own output, "Depth is estimated from the picture, and so is motion on cards without the optical flow engine. Expect some artifacts." It draws subtitles over the render "so the model never warps the text". [dlss5-video-player README](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player)

Measured A/B results on video (DLSS 5 Video Player, RTX 4080 SUPER, DLSS-NR 310.8.0, guides passed through RenoDX)
- Depth barely matters, even exact depth. With true depth, a heuristic proxy, or a constant 0.75, outputs "sit within 0.03 dB, 0.002 SSIM" of each other on two synthetic near/far clips. One signal appeared. On an interior clip, constant depth raised the false-motion rate from 0.0605 to 0.0759 while true depth matched the proxy at 0.0612. [guide files report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/guide-files-20260924/REPORT.md)
- The depth channel is live. Proxy and constant depth produce different output digests on all eight test clips, but the report finds "no visible quality win" and keeps the proxy on cost alone. [depth A/B report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/depth-ab-20260914/REPORT.md)
- That proxy is a heuristic, `0.92 - 0.42*yn - 0.17*motion - 0.10*grad` clamped to 0.08 to 0.97 with a 0.80/0.20 temporal EMA, on a 160×90 grid. A disabled depth guide is still bound as a uniform 0.75 because "the D3D12 contract rejects null resources" in that path. [depth A/B report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/depth-ab-20260914/REPORT.md)
- Motion matters. On a layered pan, a CPU block matcher cost 3.1 dB and 0.078 SSIM against NVIDIA's hardware optical flow and smeared the layers. Exact motion added only another 0.13 dB over hardware flow. "NVOFA recovers most of what exact motion is worth." [guide files report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/guide-files-20260924/REPORT.md)
- For content that does not move, all-zero motion is the exact answer. On a held frame, optical flow returned a small spurious field, and without a zero-motion test the output "creeps", with sharpness against the source climbing from 1.03x to 1.12x over two seconds and temporal sigma p99 of 2.15 luma levels against 1.41. With a test that snaps ambiguous vectors to zero, the render was byte-identical to the zero-motion render. The test costs 0.06 to 0.18 ms at 1080p. [zero-motion gate report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/neural-mv-gate-20260924/REPORT.md)
- The player's architecture notes say the neural feature "does not read a jitter offset at all", and it reads flow vectors with a nearest fetch because interpolating across a disocclusion "invents a vector no cell measured". It gates vectors with a forward/backward consistency check. [dlss5-video-player architecture](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)

### Inferences
- DLSS 5 is tolerant of a missing or flat depth input. Three independent projects run it with null, constant or heuristic depth, and the one careful measurement puts the benefit of perfect depth below 0.03 dB. Feeding Depth Anything output is low risk and also low reward for this feature.
- Those depth measurements passed through RenoDX at a 12 pixel grid pitch, so they bound what coarse depth can do, not what full-resolution depth with sharp silhouettes can do. The report says so itself.
- Motion quality is where the effort should go. The failure modes to design against are, in order of reported severity, zero vectors during camera motion (accumulating ghosting), confident wrong vectors on lighting changes, and small non-zero noise on static content (slow sharpening drift).
- A workable policy copied from these projects is hardware or GPU optical flow from current to previous on the raw captured frames, a forward/backward consistency check, a snap-to-zero test for static regions, nearest sampling, and `DLSSNR.Reset = 1` on scene cuts and after dropped frames.
- The baked-in HUD is an unsolved cost for every capture-based tool. Nobody reports using `DLSSNR.UI` or `UICorrection` successfully without a separate UI buffer.

### Gaps
- I found no public side-by-side images or metrics for "constant depth and zero motion" against "engine depth and motion" in the same game scene. The evidence is video clips and prose reports.
- The RenoDX DLSS 5 add-on is closed source, so what it does to depth and motion before feature 18 is not visible. RenoDX Discord discussion was not searchable.
- No source says what the "depth or movement data is not supported" message checks. It may be a format or size check by OptiScaler and not NVIDIA's DLL.

## What are the DLSS depth conventions, and what do Ray Reconstruction, Frame Generation and DLSS 5 require beyond Super Resolution?

### Takeaway
DLSS SR wants the engine's hardware depth at render resolution, any single-channel or depth-stencil format, near at 0 and far at 1 unless the inverted flag is set, and says only that depth helps "object tracking and pixel alignment". RR and FG add camera matrices and more buffers. DLSS 5 as integrated by NVIDIA takes depth and an inverted flag and nothing else geometric.

### Cited Findings
- Per evaluate call DLSS needs "The raw color buffer for the frame (in HDR or LDR/SDR space)", "Screen space motion vectors that are: accurate and calculated at 16 or 32 bits per-pixel; and updated each frame", "The depth buffer for the frame", and exposure if HDR. It also needs sub-pixel jitter with at least 16 phases. [DLSS Programming Guide, 31 March 2026](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- Depth format, section 3.3, is "any format with one channel in it (for instance R32_FLOAT or D32_FLOAT), as well as any depth-stencil format (for instance D24S8)". [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- Section 3.8 in full says "To assist in better object tracking and pixel alignment, DLSS uses the depth buffer generated by the engine during rasterization. The algorithm assumes that the near plane is 0.0 and the far plane is 1.0 (but this can be inverted, see below)." The flag `NVSDK_NGX_DLSS_Feature_Flags_DepthInverted` is set "if the engine uses depth with a near plane at 1.0 and far plane at 0.0". The guide says nothing about precision or about what inaccurate depth does. [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- Input buffers must be in `D3D12_RESOURCE_STATE_NON_PIXEL_SHADER_RESOURCE` because a compute shader reads them, and the output must allow unordered access. [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- Jitter offsets are in pixel space at render size, between -0.5 and +0.5, with zero meaning no jitter. [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- DLSS SR belongs before tone mapping, "as close to the start of post processing as possible". [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- On scene changes, set `InReset` non-zero for the first frame to avoid "ghost images" from the previous scene, and "Improper use of this can result in temporal flickering, heavy aliasing or other visual artifacts." [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- Ray Reconstruction accepts either kind of depth. "Either view-space depth or HW depth can be provided", it is "the same depth data used to generate motion vector data", and `InUseHWDepth` selects hardware depth. RR also needs diffuse and specular albedo, normals, roughness, and either specular motion vectors or specular hit distance with world-to-view and view-to-clip matrices. [DLSS-RR Integration Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS-RR%20Integration%20Guide.pdf)
- Frame Generation wants "The depth of the current frame, specified in hardware/non-linear units", with `depthInverted` set as needed, and its resolution need not match the backbuffer. It also requires `clipToPrevClip` and `prevClipToClip`, view-to-clip matrices, camera position and basis vectors, `cameraNear`, `cameraFar`, FOV and aspect ratio. Hudless color and UI alpha are optional but recommended. [DLSS-FG Programming Guide, 310.7.0](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS-FG%20Programming%20Guide.pdf)
- FG has a debug mode that "validates the correctness of the input camera matrices relative to the provided depth and motion vector textures" by highlighting static geometry whose motion vector disagrees with depth reprojection. [DLSS-FG Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS-FG%20Programming%20Guide.pdf)
- Streamline tags depth as `kBufferTypeDepth` with the note "Must be suitable to use with clipToPrevClip transformation", offers `kBufferTypeLinearDepth` as an alternative ("Required either this or kBufferTypeDepth"), and carries `depthInverted`, `cameraNear`, `cameraFar`, `clipToPrevClip`, `jitterOffset`, `mvecScale`, `cameraMotionIncluded`, `motionVectors3D`, `motionVectorsDilated`, `motionVectorsJittered` and `reset` in its common constants. [Streamline ProgrammingGuide.md](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/docs/ProgrammingGuide.md), [sl_core_types.h](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/include/sl_core_types.h)
- For DLSS 5, NVIDIA's Remix integration passes `pInDepth` with `InDepthInverted = 0`, the depth subrect, and no near, far, matrices or jitter. It reuses the same depth it gives DLSS SR or RR. [dxvk-remix rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp)
- A reverse-engineered Stray add-on found that a typeless planar depth format (`r32_g8_typeless`) is the likely cause of `FAIL_UnsupportedInputFormat` from the DLSS-NR DLL. [stray-dlssnr](https://github.com/lunks/stray-dlssnr)

### Inferences
- DLSS 5 cannot linearize depth metrically, because it never receives near and far planes or a projection matrix. It can only use depth as a relative signal such as edges, ordering and frame-to-frame consistency along motion vectors. That is consistent with the measured insensitivity to depth values.
- For Unishade, `R32_FLOAT` at the color resolution is the safest depth format. It is what the Feeder sends and what the SR guide lists first. Avoid typeless formats.
- Depth does not need to match color jitter in Unishade's case because there is no jitter at all. In engine integrations depth is naturally jittered with the color since both come from the same rasterization.
- Whether the DLL accepts a null `DLSSNR.Depth` on D3D12 is uncertain. A Vulkan layer leaves it null, while a D3D12 player always binds a flat texture. Binding a real texture is the safer choice.

### Gaps
- NVIDIA does not document what DLSS SR or DLSS 5 does with depth internally. Uses such as disocclusion detection and motion vector dilation along depth edges are common knowledge about temporal upscalers but the DLSS guide does not state them.
- No source says whether DLSS 5 treats a constant depth buffer as "no information" or as a flat plane.

## What are the DLSS motion vector conventions, and what goes wrong with zero or wrong vectors?

### Takeaway
The convention is the same across DLSS SR, FG and, by NVIDIA's Remix code and community agreement, DLSS 5. Vectors point from the current pixel to its previous position, in pixels of the motion vector texture after multiplying by MVecScale, origin top-left with +Y down, in RG16F or RG32F. NVIDIA calls bad motion vectors "the most common cause of visual artifacts".

### Cited Findings
- "The motion vectors map a pixel from the current frame to its position in the previous frame. That is, when the motion vector for the pixel is added to the pixel's current location, the result is the location the pixel occupied in the previous frame." [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- "IMPORTANT: Incorrect or poor precision motion vectors are the most common cause of visual artifacts when DLSS is enabled." [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- Format is `RG32_FLOAT` or `RG16_FLOAT`, X in red and Y in green. Values are "the number of pixels calculated in screen space (ie the amount a pixel has moved at the render resolution)", with [0,0] at the upper left. They can be negative and sub-pixel. Custom encodings "must be decoded before calling DLSS". [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- `MVLowRes` says vectors are at render resolution, the preferred case. "if standard input resolution motion vectors are sent they do not need to be dilated, DLSS dilates them internally. If display resolution motion vectors are sent, they must be dilated." `MVJittered` says the vectors include jitter and DLSS subtracts it. [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- `InMVScaleX/Y` exist for vectors that point the other way or are in UV space. They "should be set to 1.0 if motion vectors do not need to be scaled and should never be 0.0f". [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- Engines that only write vectors for moving geometry need a resolve pass that fills camera motion from depth for every other pixel. The guide gives a shader for it. [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- DLSS "may then 'erase' a particular feature or can produce a 'ghost' or trail behind a moving feature" on small particles, animated or scrolling textures, very thin objects, "Objects with missing motion vectors (many particle effects)", and disocclusions with very large vectors. [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- FG states the unit rule most precisely. The value is the distance to the previous position "in pixels at the resolution of the motion vector resource". It requires a "Dense motion vector field (i.e. includes camera motion, and motion of dynamic objects)". [DLSS-FG Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS-FG%20Programming%20Guide.pdf), [Streamline ProgrammingGuideDLSS_G.md](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS_G.md)
- Streamline uses a different scale convention. `mvecScale` must bring values into the -1 to 1 range, so pixel-space vectors use `{1/renderWidth, 1/renderHeight}`. This applies to the Streamline API, not to raw NGX. [Streamline ProgrammingGuideDLSS_G.md](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS_G.md)
- For DLSS 5 through raw NGX, NVIDIA's Remix code passes render-resolution vectors "in render-pixel units" with scale 1, 1 and gives the motion texture its own subrect size. [dxvk-remix rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_dlss_neural_rendering.cpp)
- Community DLSS 5 callers agree with that. One passes normalized UV with scale (width, height), another passes pixels with scale 1, both current to previous in 16-bit float RG. [ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR architecture](https://github.com/lisitskyaa/ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR/blob/HEAD/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), [DLSS5VKLayer](https://github.com/bmitch87/DLSS5VKLayer)
- UE4's velocity buffer is a normalized integer texture with a scale and a bias, which `MVecScaleX/Y` "can never remove", so it must be decoded first. [stray-dlssnr](https://github.com/lunks/stray-dlssnr)

### Inferences
- Unishade's `R16G16_FLOAT` texture is the right format. Write `prev_pos - cur_pos` in pixels with +X right and +Y down and set both scales to 1. If the flow runs at lower resolution than the color, either store vectors in flow-texture pixels and set the `MVec` subrect size, as Remix does, or upsample and rescale to color pixels. Remix's code implies the DLL interprets values in motion-texture pixels, but only NVIDIA's usage shows this, no document does.
- DLSS 5 has no `MVLowRes` or `MVJittered` flag in any integration I read, so assume it does its own handling and expects undilated, unjittered vectors.
- Optical flow naturally gives a dense field that includes camera motion, so the "dense motion vector" requirement is met. What flow gets wrong is occlusion boundaries, repeated texture, transparency, lighting change and anything the HUD overlaps.
- Half-float precision is ample. 16-bit float resolves far below a hundredth of a pixel at 4K magnitudes.

### Gaps
- No NVIDIA text confirms the sign, unit or dilation expectations of `DLSSNR.MVec`. A sign error would look like doubled ghosting during pans, so Unishade should test with a known synthetic pan.
- How large a vector error DLSS 5 tolerates before its history rejects or ghosts is unmeasured. The only numbers are the 3.1 dB gap between a coarse block matcher and hardware flow on one synthetic clip.

## How do FSR and XeSS compare on depth and motion requirements?

### Takeaway
The conventions are close to identical across vendors. All want current-to-previous screen-space vectors at render resolution in a 2-channel float format with a scale parameter, and a depth buffer with an inverted flag. They differ mostly in how much they use depth. XeSS only needs it to dilate low-res vectors.

### Cited Findings
- FSR's depth input is "APPLICATION SPECIFIED (1x FLOAT)" at render resolution, with `FFX_UPSCALE_ENABLE_DEPTH_INVERTED` for a "[max..0]" buffer and `FFX_UPSCALE_ENABLE_DEPTH_INFINITE` for an infinite far plane. AMD strongly recommends "an inverted, infinite depth buffer". [FidelityFX SDK FSR upscaler doc](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/main/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md)
- FSR's debug checker warns when a "cameraFar value is very low which may result in depth separation artifacting", which shows FSR uses camera near and far to interpret depth. [FidelityFX SDK FSR upscaler doc](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/main/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md)
- FSR motion vectors are "APPLICATION SPECIFIED (2x FLOAT)", screen-space pixels in the range "[<-width, -height> ... <width, height>]", from the current pixel to its previous position, with `motionVectorScale` for other units, render resolution by default with `FFX_UPSCALE_ENABLE_DISPLAY_RESOLUTION_MOTION_VECTORS` as the alternative, and unjittered unless `FFX_UPSCALE_ENABLE_MOTION_VECTORS_JITTER_CANCELLATION` is set. [FidelityFX SDK FSR upscaler doc](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/main/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md)
- FSR wants "all opaque, alpha-tested and alpha-blended objects" to write motion vectors, and says drawing objects without vectors into the reactive mask "can decrease temporal ghosting". The reactive mask and the transparency and composition mask are both `R8_UNORM`. [FidelityFX SDK FSR upscaler doc](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/main/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md)
- XeSS takes low-res vectors by default or high-res with `XESS_INIT_FLAG_HIGH_RES_MV`, and NDC units with `XESS_INIT_FLAG_USE_NDC_VELOCITY`. "Low-res motion vectors are not dilated". [XeSS SR developer guide](https://github.com/intel/xess/blob/main/doc/xess_sr_developer_guide_english.md)
- "If XeSS-SR is used with low-res motion vectors, it also requires a depth texture for velocity dilation. Any depth format, such as D32_FLOAT or D24_UNORM, is supported." Smaller is nearer by default, and `XESS_INIT_FLAG_INVERTED_DEPTH` flips that. [XeSS SR developer guide](https://github.com/intel/xess/blob/main/doc/xess_sr_developer_guide_english.md)
- XeSS says errors in visibility, shading or the motion vector "typically results in ghosting artifacts", and offers a responsive pixel mask, clamped internally at 0.8, for objects without valid vectors. [XeSS SR developer guide](https://github.com/intel/xess/blob/main/doc/xess_sr_developer_guide_english.md)
- OptiScaler notes a practical consequence. Unreal's XeSS plugin "does not provide depth", so swapping XeSS inputs into other upscalers breaks them. [OptiScaler README, DLSSNR fork](https://github.com/wilsjo2/OptiScaler-DLSSNR-PreSR-Multipass)

### Inferences
- A depth and motion pair that satisfies DLSS conventions also satisfies FSR and XeSS after setting flags, so one estimator in Unishade can feed any of them. The Magpie fork already feeds DLSS SR, FSR 2/3/4 and XeSS from captured frames and estimated motion.
- XeSS with high-res vectors is the one mainstream upscaler that needs no depth at all, which is another sign that depth is a secondary input for this family of algorithms.
- FSR is the most depth-dependent of the three, since it asks for near, far and FOV. A monocular depth map with invented near and far is most likely to cause trouble there.

### Gaps
- The FSR page I read does not describe how depth drives disocclusion detection. Older FSR 2 documentation covers depth clip and reconstructed previous depth, and I did not re-read it for this note.
- FSR 4's ML upscaler may weigh inputs differently from FSR 2/3. I found no document on its sensitivity to bad depth.

## Do shipping products prove that estimated motion and no engine depth can be good enough?

### Takeaway
Yes for motion, with visible limits. Driver-level frame generation and every capture-based DLSS 5 tool run on optical flow alone, and reviewers describe the result as usable but more artifact-prone around fast motion and HUDs. Estimated depth has little evidence either way because most of these products do not use depth at all.

### Cited Findings
- NVIDIA Smooth Motion is driver-level frame generation that "can't tap into engine data or motion vectors", and its lack of motion vectors and depth "makes it more prone to visual artifacts than DLSS FG", especially around fast-moving objects and HUD elements (search summary). [XDA Developers](https://www.xda-developers.com/this-nvidia-app-feature-brings-frame-generation-to-games-that-never-supported-it/)
- AMD AFMF has no motion vector input and interpolates between two finished frames. It turns itself off during heavy camera motion, and AFMF 2.1 adds repeat-frame and blended-frame fallbacks for fast motion (search summary). [Club386](https://www.club386.com/amd-afmf-2-1-will-let-you-choose-between-smooth-and-quality-frame-generation/)
- NVIDIA's own DLSS Frame Generation mixes both kinds of input. One model uses "game data such as motion vectors and depth", and a second calculates optical flow for UI and ray-traced effects that engine vectors miss. [NVIDIA GeForce, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/dlss-5-faster-ai-model-hogwarts-legacy-rtx-spark-laptops/)
- NVIDIA hardware optical flow (NVOFA) works on a 2×2 pixel grid with S10.5 fixed point output, so one vector covers four pixels at 1/32 pixel precision, and it runs on Turing and later cards. [dlss5-video-player architecture](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md)
- On DLSS 5 specifically, hardware optical flow recovered nearly all the benefit of exact motion (0.13 dB short on a synthetic pan), while a coarse CPU block matcher lost 3.1 dB. [guide files report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/guide-files-20260924/REPORT.md)
- Games-oriented temporal upscalers do badly on video. Against bicubic on six clips, DLSS Super Resolution "scored lower on all of them", at 74.9 VMAF against bicubic's 80.5 and RTX Video Super Resolution's 87.5. [dlss5-video-player README](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player)
- NeuralScreen describes desktop DLSS frame generation with "the depth is flat and the motion is estimated, there is no engine cooperation, so UI and text can distort — the known cost of the approach". [NeuralScreen](https://github.com/perseval-BLR/NeuralScreen)

### Inferences
- The quality bar for motion is roughly "GPU optical flow at 2 to 4 pixel grid with sub-pixel precision". A block matcher on a 12 pixel grid is measurably too coarse.
- DLSS SR on unjittered captured frames is a poor fit, since it was trained to integrate jittered samples. DLSS 5 does not depend on jitter, which is why it transfers to video and capture far better than SR does.
- Video super-resolution networks that estimate flow internally, such as the BasicVSR family, and RTX Video Super Resolution show that learned temporal models can work from color alone. I did not fetch sources for them in this session, so treat this as background knowledge.
- For Unishade on NVIDIA hardware, NVOFA through the Optical Flow SDK's D3D12 interface is the proven motion source. A compute-shader Lucas-Kanade or hierarchical block matcher is the fallback others use.

### Gaps
- I found no source for how RTX Video Super Resolution or RTX Video HDR work internally, or whether they use motion at all.
- No published comparison rates optical flow methods (NVOFA, RAFT-class networks, block matching) by DLSS 5 output quality beyond the single report above.
- Academic neural supersampling work that takes depth and motion as inputs was not reviewed here.

## How should a relative monocular depth map be turned into something that behaves like a hardware depth buffer?

### Takeaway
Normalized inverse depth from a monocular network is already an affine image of a reversed-Z hardware depth buffer, so the conversion is a per-sequence normalization and a flag. For DLSS 5 the exact mapping hardly matters. Temporal steadiness and edge alignment with the color frame matter more than metric accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- DLSS assumes "the near plane is 0.0 and the far plane is 1.0" unless the inverted flag says near is 1.0 and far is 0.0. [DLSS Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- FG specifies depth "in hardware/non-linear units". RR accepts "Either view-space depth or HW depth". Streamline has a separate linear depth tag. [DLSS-FG Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS-FG%20Programming%20Guide.pdf), [DLSS-RR Integration Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS-RR%20Integration%20Guide.pdf), [sl_core_types.h](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Streamline/blob/main/include/sl_core_types.h)
- NVIDIA's DLSS 5 integration passes hardware-style depth with `InDepthInverted = 0` and no camera constants. [dxvk-remix rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp](https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/dxvk-remix/blob/main/src/dxvk/rtx_render/rtx_ngx_wrapper.cpp)
- The Feeder makes inversion a setting that follows ReShade's `RESHADE_DEPTH_INPUT_IS_REVERSED`, and its add-on "warns when sampled depth is flat without disabling the feed". [DLSS5-Feeder](https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder)
- A shipped video tool uses a made-up depth with values clamped to 0.08 to 0.97 and smooths it over time with a 0.80/0.20 EMA. With exact depth substituted, output changed by at most 0.03 dB. [depth A/B report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/depth-ab-20260914/REPORT.md), [guide files report](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/docs/measurements/guide-files-20260924/REPORT.md)
- A cost-scaling proxy for DLSS-NR uses native depth for a "Depth-Aware Bilateral Silhouette Preservation" step that guards silhouettes when compositing a lower-resolution neural result. [DLSSNR-Cost-Scaler](https://github.com/xenmods/DLSSNR-Cost-Scaler)
- FG's matrix test mode checks that static geometry's motion vectors agree with reprojecting depth through `clipToPrevClip`. [DLSS-FG Programming Guide](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS-FG%20Programming%20Guide.pdf)

### Inferences
- The math. A standard perspective depth buffer stores `z_hw = (1/n - 1/z) / (1/n - 1/f)`, which is linear in inverse view distance `1/z`. Reversed-Z stores `1 - z_hw`, and with an infinite far plane it is `n/z`. Depth Anything V2's relative output is inverse depth up to an unknown scale and shift, larger meaning nearer. That last point is from the model's paper and the sibling notes, not re-verified here. So `s = clamp((d - d_far) / (d_near - d_far), 0, 1)` is a valid reversed-Z buffer. Pass `s` with `DLSSNR.DepthInverted = 1`, or pass `1 - s` with `DepthInverted = 0`. No near or far plane has to be invented for DLSS 5 because it never receives one.
- Choose `d_near` and `d_far` from robust percentiles, for example 1st and 99th, and smooth them over time. Per-frame min-max makes the whole buffer pulse when one object enters the frame, and that pulse is a frame-to-frame depth change with no matching motion. Reset the smoothing on scene cuts along with `DLSSNR.Reset`.
- If a consumer needs linear view depth, as RR's view-space option or an FSR path with near and far would, pick `n` and `f`, compute `1/z = 1/f + s·(1/n - 1/f)` and invert. The unknown shift in the network output means the far end is unreliable, so sky may land at a finite depth. Clamping the lowest values to exactly far fixes sky.
- Geometric consistency between depth and motion only becomes a hard requirement for consumers that reproject depth with camera matrices, such as FG and FSR's disocclusion logic. Unishade has no matrices to give, DLSS 5 does not take them, and so the depth-versus-motion consistency check that exists in engines cannot even be evaluated by DLSS 5. What can still hurt is depth edges that sit a few pixels off the color edges, or depth that flickers. Keep depth edges snapped to color edges with a joint bilateral upsample, and filter it temporally along the estimated motion.
- Depth Anything V2 Small at reduced resolution will be softer than the frame. The 12 pixel grid experiment suggests DLSS 5 will not punish that.
- A useful secondary role for the estimated depth is inside Unishade's own motion validation, the way the Feeder uses depth as one of four tests to reject bad flow vectors.

### Gaps
- Nobody has published DLSS 5 results with a learned monocular depth map. The one project that planned it could not run the model and only measured the exact-depth ceiling.
- Whether the scale of the depth range matters to DLSS 5, for example a scene compressed into 0.4 to 0.6 against one spread over 0 to 1, is untested.
- Whether full-resolution sharp depth changes the result is open. The existing measurements used coarse depth.

## What do NVIDIA's licence terms say about redistributing the NGX DLLs and using the feature outside approved integrations?

### Takeaway
The public DLSS SDK licence lets an application ship SDK binaries in object-code form under conditions, but `nvngx_dlssnr.dll` is not in the public SDK, so no public licence grants redistribution of it. The licence also forbids reverse engineering, bypassing technical limits and pulling the SDK under an open-source licence. This is a reading of the text, not legal advice.

### Cited Findings
- The licence grants the right to "Distribute any software and materials within the SDK, other than developer tools provided for your internal use, as incorporated in object code format into a software application subject to the distribution requirements". [NVIDIA DLSS SDK LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)
- "You may not reverse engineer, decompile or disassemble, or remove copyright or other proprietary notices from any portion of the SDK", and "you may not distribute or sublicense the SDK as a stand-alone product." [NVIDIA DLSS SDK LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)
- "You may not bypass, disable, or circumvent any technical limitation, encryption, security, digital rights management or authentication mechanism in the SDK." [NVIDIA DLSS SDK LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)
- "You may not use the SDK in any manner that would cause it to become subject to an open source software license", with licences requiring source disclosure or free redistribution of the SDK as examples. [NVIDIA DLSS SDK LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)
- The RTX supplement says "the DLSS SDK, NGX SDK and RTX Dynamic Vibrance SDK are licensed for you to develop applications only for their use in systems with NVIDIA GPUs". Distributors must pass on terms "at least as protective" as the licence. [NVIDIA DLSS SDK LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/LICENSE.txt)
- The public SDK contains `nvngx_dlss.dll`, `nvngx_dlssd.dll` and `nvngx_dlssg.dll` material and no DLSS 5 header, document or release note. [NVIDIA/DLSS releases](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/releases)
- Community tools describe their own position candidly. One says its NR runtime is a "modified, unsigned community build" and that NVIDIA's signed build "was not available from an approved source". Another calls its copy "the leaked 310.8.0 build" and offers to remove it if rights holders ask. A third ships no NVIDIA DLLs and tells users "Obtaining and using the NVIDIA runtime is your responsibility under NVIDIA's terms." [dlss5-video-player THIRD_PARTY](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player/blob/HEAD/THIRD_PARTY.md), [NeuralScreen](https://github.com/perseval-BLR/NeuralScreen), [video2dlssnr](https://github.com/DaniilSokolyuk/video2dlssnr)
- The 2600th player warns that "re-uploads of DLSS 5 tools under other accounts have been reported carrying malware". [dlss5-video-player README](https://github.com/2600th/dlss5-video-player)
- Press reports describe the RTX 40 patch of the leaked DLL and found no takedown or DMCA action, and they disagree on whether NVIDIA has commented (search summary). [VideoCardz](https://videocardz.com/newz/leaked-dlss-5-already-working-with-rtx-40-series)

### Inferences
- The lowest-risk posture for an MIT project such as Unishade is the one video2dlssnr and ComfyUI-DLSS5-NR take. Ship no NVIDIA binaries, load `nvngx_dlssnr.dll` from a path the user supplies, keep NVIDIA headers and DLLs outside the MIT grant, and say plainly that the feature is unofficial.
- Version 310.8.0.0 is the build that leaked from the NBA 2K27 early-access files. A user who owns a DLSS 5 game has a legitimately delivered copy on disk, but nothing in the public licence authorizes using it from another application.
- Patched DLLs that run on RTX 20 to 40 cards fall under the "bypass ... any technical limitation" clause for anyone bound by the SDK licence. Unishade should not ship or recommend them. An unpatched signed DLL limits the add-on to RTX 50 hardware.
- Parameter names learned from other projects' reverse engineering are the weak point of every project in this space, Unishade included. The risk falls if NVIDIA publishes the DLSS 5 headers that dxvk-remix already includes.
- Driver updates can break direct NGX callers. One tool reports that from driver 616.64 the NGX core "routes feature 18 into snippet 310.8.0.0 itself" and faults on the older call sequence, and another reports add-on versions failing on 616.64 and later. Expect to maintain this path.

### Gaps
- I did not find a licence or EULA text that covers `nvngx_dlssnr.dll` specifically, nor the terms under which game developers receive it.
- NVIDIA's position on third-party use of feature 18 outside games is not stated in any primary source I found.
- I did not read the NVIDIA driver licence or the RTX Remix licence for terms on loading NGX snippets from other applications.
